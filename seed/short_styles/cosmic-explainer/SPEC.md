# Style study: flat-vector "science explainer" Shorts (six reference Shorts, measured)

References (names withheld): ref-A kitchen poisons (67.2 s), ref-B a virus we almost beat (78.5 s), ref-C the most average person (70.7 s),
ref-D a drug and the brain (71.7 s), ref-E what poverty costs (72.2 s), ref-F running out of water (78.3 s). All 1080x1920, 60 fps.
Measured by the stylelab tools; `EYE_NOTES.md` corrections win where a detector misread this style (captions, heads, camera holds).

## 1. What the style is, and the numbers that define it
A narrated explainer Short about one big "how does this really work / what is the hidden truth" question, drawn as **flat vector
illustration with no outlines**: solid fills, one darker same-hue shadow shape, saturated high-key colour on gradient grounds, cute
round characters and talking objects, glowing "inside the body" diagrams, chunky data labels. Think "printed sticker on a glowing screen".
One idea per picture, a new picture every 2-3 s, always moving inside the picture, never a real photo.

| Measure | Value (six refs) | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Length | 67-79 s (median 72) | Long Short: needs ~15-18 pictures |
| Words per second | 2.6-3.1 (median 2.9), 201-219 words | Dense, no filler; the voice never rests |
| Cuts (hard + dissolve) | 20-47 per Short; median shot 2.1-3.0 s in 5 of 6 (ref-D is a dance-like 0.29 s on one sequence) | One new picture per sentence, not per word |
| Cuts in first 3 s | 1-3 | The hook is ONE strong picture, not a montage |
| Near-still frames | 37-66 % (median ~55 %) | Holds are common: life is inside the frame |
| Camera during holds | locked, 0.0 % drift, 0.0 zoom | Measured: no slow push-in here |
| Camera moves | fast only: p90 0.4-1.5 % of width/frame, p99 2.9-8.3 % | Whips/zooms are punctuation, short 0.13-0.53 s |
| Captions on screen | 99.5 % of frames | Always while the voice speaks |
| Loudness | -11.8 to -13.7 LUFS, LRA 1.4-1.9 | Loud, flat, phone-ready |
| Grain / vignette | none / very light | Clean vector surface |

## 2. Script formula
- **Length:** 200-220 words for 70-80 s. For a 45 s Short aim for ~130 words, 60 s ~175 words (always ~2.9 words/s).
- **Hook (0-3 s, 6-12 words):** the title as a shocking claim or a plain big question, said from the very first word (first word at 0.0 s in
  all six; no "hey guys", no intro). Patterns: "[Trusted everyday thing] killed you!", "Why [unfair thing] is [surprising]",
  "Could [defeated danger] return?", "Who is the world's most [odd superlative] person?". WHY: the question is the promise; viewers stay to hear the answer.
- **Sentences:** 5-12 words, one fact each; a sentence = one picture. Plain spoken English, concrete nouns and numbers ("500 teaspoons", "20 g",
  "~$10 a gram"), a little dark or dry humour, never jargon without an instant picture for it.
- **Shape (time of a 72 s Short):** 0-3 hook claim -> 3-15 first suspect / first cause shown concretely -> 15-45 explanation chain
  (cause -> mechanism -> consequence), each link a new picture, numbers escalate -> ~55-60 % turn ("But the deadliest...", "But here's the
  catch..."), the picture from the hook returns -> 50-65 absurd or hopeful scale-up (a map, a crowd, a giant number) -> 65-72 payoff that
  lands on the opening image or a cheeky mascot, hopeful or ironic ("it's cheaper than doing nothing").
- **Bookend:** the hook picture returns as the last or second-last shot (ref-A: shot #0, #13, #17).
- **Caveat line** where a claim needs it ("According to rat studies, human responses may differ"), shown in a navy box: it makes the style
  feel trustworthy and is part of its look.
- Ends without "subscribe". Last word is the payoff; ~1.1 s of music tail after it.

## 3. Voice and sound
- **Narrator:** calm, friendly, slightly playful adult voice, f0 about 121-129 Hz, steady. Voice level about -13 dB RMS (-12.5 to -14.5).
- **Pace:** 2.6-3.1 words/s; median gap between words 0.0 s, 89 % of gaps under 60 ms, p90 gap 0.0-0.29 s. Sentence-end pauses are
  0.3-0.65 s (about 20 per Short, always after a punchy last word like "lethal.", "cheaper.", "grenade."): they are where picture cuts land.
- **Music bed and sound effects:** see section 15 (measured on 16 Shorts from separated stems; it replaces the first, rougher reading
  that called the bed "8-18 dB under" and counted 1.7-3 hits a second: those hits were the music's own notes, not effects).
- No laughter, no stingers, no vocal reactions.

## 4. Pacing
- Shot length median 2.1-3.0 s, p90 about 5-7 s, longest hold 6-13 s only for one big diagram while a long sentence plays.
- **Hook:** one picture held 0.6-2.5 s, then 1-2 quick cuts; ref-A swaps three suspects at ~0.75 s inside the hook, the others hold one title picture.
- **Transitions:** mostly hard cut exactly on a sentence start (about 60 %); about 25 % are quick dissolves or whip pans of 0.13-0.5 s; a few are
  a zoom into a "zoom bubble", a morph (one object becomes the next), or one single white flash frame at a big reveal. Never a slow cross-fade.
- **Inside a shot** something always moves: labels pop (overshoot is rare, see 14), pills stack one by one on the words, counters roll up,
  a ring grows, one thing acts (14), characters blink / bob / breathe. Motion per half-second is 1-12 (of 255) in holds and 60-200 in a move.
- Mood rhythm: bright warm scenes (people, places, jokes) alternate with dark navy/indigo scenes (body, space, data, death) every 2-4 shots.

## 5. Camera
- Flat 2D, no 3D, no tilt. **Locked during holds** (measured). All camera motion is a fast move between beats:
  whip pan (0.13-0.35 s, p99 speed up to 8 % of width/frame, rarely 22-47 % on a hard whip), zoom-in toward a point (0.3-0.5 s),
  punch-in/scale-pop of one element (0.2-0.3 s).
- Easing: accelerate quickly, land soft (peak speed reached at 25-65 % of the move, then decelerates); a tiny overshoot (3-6 %) on
  popped elements, none on the camera.
- Parallax between background and foreground layers only inside whips and zooms. No roll, no handheld jitter, no shake.
- WHY: a locked frame keeps the flat illustration crisp and readable; the fast moves feel like a page flipping.

## 6. Composition and framing
- Vertical 9:16, **one focal point per frame**, centred or on the upper third (centre of mass at 36-44 % of frame height on explanation shots).
- The bottom ~20 % is kept clear for captions (caption centre at 78.4 % of height).
- Shot sizes: close-up face (hook, emotion), medium figure from waist up (people), hero object centred and large (about 45-65 % of frame width),
  full-bleed diagrams (body, map, crowd grid), top-down table scenes for props.
- Characters are cropped by the frame edge on purpose when close; wide shots are rare. A standing figure always has a flat darker ellipse
  shadow under it (about 25 % alpha).
- Depth: layered flat planes in nearly every shot (see section 14): a far plane hazed toward the ground colour and softly blurred,
  the crisp subject, and near-lens shapes (dark crowd heads, big soft bokeh discs, washed-out blurred props) in front. Pushes and
  whips move the planes at their own speeds.
- Zoom bubble: a thick yellow ring (about 6 px stroke at 1080) magnifies the micro world, placed in the upper half over the thing it explains, grows from the point it looks at.

## 7. The look
- **No outlines anywhere** on characters and props: shape = solid fill + one darker same-hue shadow shape + sometimes one lighter highlight strip.
  Rounded corners everywhere. (Title text and some pills carry a thin dark outline.)
- **Palette (hex, sampled from grounds and fills):**
  yellow #FFD23F, orange #FF7A1A, hot magenta #FF3CAC, violet #B13CFF, electric blue #2B4BFF, cyan #00C8FF, aqua-teal #19D6B8,
  deep navy #0B0A3C, warm skin range #F2C9A0 / #C98B5A / #8A5A3C / #5B3A29, rosy cheek #FF8FA3, label yellow #FFE600, danger red #E5262B.
  Saturation about 0.66-0.88, brightness 0.57-0.93 per Short: high-key, saturated, never grey, never beige, never pastel-dull.
- **Grounds:** one vertical linear gradient per scene between two saturated neighbours (sky-blue to cyan, purple to magenta, orange to yellow, teal to
  green, indigo to near-black blue with a soft radial glow behind the subject). White only as ONE deliberate flash frame.
- **Light:** comes from glow, not lamps: a radial cyan/white burst behind a hero for highs, bokeh discs and neon rims on dark scenes, a soft vignette
  on purples. Heroes standing on a backdrop disc or wall cast their WHOLE silhouette onto it, offset down-right, in the backdrop's
  darker tone (section 14); figures on open ground get the flat floor ellipse.
- **Detail density:** low and smooth, no grain or texture; interest from hue shifts. Rooms are built from big flat blocks (cabinet, table, window), 5-8 objects max.
- **Busy-ness:** 2-3 hue families cover 80 % of a frame. Outfit hue is far from the set hue (about 170 degrees apart) so the figure pops.
- **Warm/cool logic:** warm yellow/orange for danger and people beats, cool cyan/blue/teal for explanation and science beats, dark indigo for the body, space and death.

## 8. Characters
- **People:** big head (about 1/4 of body height) shaped as a tall U in ONE flat skin colour, big half-disc ears a shade deeper, NO nose,
  no blush; two dark dot eyes (#2A0A4A) with NO catchlight in medium shots (close-ups: white eyes with a coloured iris and one
  catchlight); muted capsule brows (hair mixed with skin); a tiny dark-red capsule mouth (#A0142A); hair as a dome with flame-shaped
  locks; a visible deep-red neck with a chin shadow, a lighter collar trim; arms a touch lighter than the face; hands with fingers. Skin tones vary
  widely across the cast. They act with the whole body: slump, sweat drops, arms up, eyes closed in bliss, X-eyes when dead (X over one eye).
- **Talking objects (the signature):** an everyday object gets two huge white oval eyes with a dark pupil block, thin noodle arms, white mitten gloves
  and red sneakers, posed or dancing in a round spotlight oval against a dark ground, with a yellow tape-banner name label.
- **Cute organs and creatures:** a pink brain with big sparkly eyes, a white lab mouse, a mascot bird that bookends the end. Our own mascot must be original.
- **Crowds:** one tiny figure repeated in a tight grid that fills the frame (people as statistics), split into two colours to show a share.
- **Faces in close-up** are big (a face fills 60-90 % of frame width) with a clear single emotion; expression changes on the sentence, not mid-word.
- WHY: simple faces read at phone size and let any topic feel friendly even when the subject is death or money.

## 9. Sets and props
- Place scenes (kitchen, desert, hospital front, train, office, club) are 3-6 big flat shapes plus 3-5 small props; shown as a fragment, never a full room.
- **Inside-the-body scenes:** dark blue/purple body silhouette with glowing organs, pipes of light, red vein lines, yellow dots moving through the gut,
  cutaway head with skull and brain, a molecule drawn as a pink/teal ball-and-stick inside a ring.
- **Data scenes:** pill labels stacked beside a character, pie chart that fills slice by slice with % labels, counters that roll up, flat glowing world map with
  dots and a counter above, fill meters, calendars with X marks, a scale showing "20 g", a grid of 100+ tiny figures or bottles standing for a quantity.
- **Metaphor scenes:** one big literal visual idea (a debt snake eating money, a spiral trap, a safety net with people falling through, a bomb made of a banana).
- Recurring motifs: the hook victim/scene returns at the turn and the end; one chalk body outline; the yellow zoom ring; tape banners.
- Hidden jokes: tiny funny props (a banana with sunglasses, a tiny bird sweeping up the mess).

## 10. On-screen text and captions
- **Captions:** white, rounded geometric sans (medium-bold), sentence case WITH punctuation, a thin dark outline and a soft dark drop shadow.
  Fixed line at **78.4 % of frame height** (p10-p90 78.4-78.8 %), centred at 50.0 % of width, one line, never wraps, width p50 36 % of frame width
  (p10 23 %, p90 55 %). Letter band about 38 px at 1920 (2.0 % of height, 2.8 % with ascenders). 2-5 words per caption, switching with the word
  start (offset about 0 s), on screen 99.5 % of frames.
- **Caption font:** a rounded geometric sans at semi-bold weight (Fredoka SemiBold), size ~59 in Legion's caption units, white, outline 2,
  shadow 2, centred on both sides.
- **Title card (hook), labels, pills, numbers:** see section 15 (the designed lockup, small attached tags, at most one tape banner).
- Never more than one text element fighting the caption; the caption band stays clear.

## 11. The hook (first 2 seconds)
Frame 0 already shows the answer to "what is this about": a title card as the claim, over the victim or hero picture, ground a strong gradient.
- 0.0 s: title text on screen, the first word starts, hero image already complete (no fade-in from black, no empty frame).
- 0.5-2.0 s: either hold the same picture, or three fast "suspect" swaps at about 0.75 s on one object type.
- 2-3.5 s: first real cut into the story (the culprit as a talking object, or the first fact), a whip or hard cut on the first sentence end.
- Hook types seen: impossible claim, odd superlative question, "what if the danger comes back", "the hidden cost".
- WHY: the picture and the words say the same thing at the same time, so the viewer understands in under 1 s and has an open question.

## 12. KEEP vs CHANGE
**KEEP (identical):** flat no-outline vector look; gradient grounds and glow; talking objects with huge eyes; zoom ring; pill labels and tape banners;
dark/bright alternation; one idea per picture; shot length 2-3 s; locked camera with fast whips; caption position, size and treatment;
script shape (hook, chain, turn, absurd scale-up, payoff, bookend); 2.9 words/s; music and sfx levels.
**CHANGE (the creator's own):** topic, words, every character, mascot, creature and object design, exact palette tweak and accent, fonts,
sound and music, the numbers and facts, the channel name and logo.

## 13. DON'T
- Never outlines on characters, never 3D, never photos or real footage, never realistic faces.
- No slow push-in during holds, no handheld shake, no 3D tilt.
- No random gradients on top of the scene, no everything-fading-in, no glow on text, no particle bursts for decoration, no fake UI,
  no two things fighting for attention.
- No long cross-fades, no black frames, no empty first frame, no "subscribe" or "like and follow".
- No sponsor lines, no reference mascot, face, brand or title text (a small sponsor pill in some refs is NOT part of the style).
- Do not copy a reference's words, titles or characters; copy the patterns only.
- No real person's likeness, no real minors in harm scenes.

## 14. Deep study: motion, construction and the small details (measured; wins over earlier sections where they differ)
Measured frame by frame (pop_measure, transition study, idle_measure with optical flow on 65 held shots, close-up crops of
people, objects and organs). The flat engine (`flatkit/engine.js`) already does every number here; use its helpers.

**Entrances (57 measured, 7.8 a minute).** Kinds: appear/snap 37 %, slide 37 %, grow 14 %, pop 7 %. Durations: snap 4 frames
(30 fps), slide ~10, grow/pop ~12. Every move peaks in speed by frame ~3, then each frame covers ~16 % of the way left (decay
0.84 a frame): fast arrival, soft landing, never a constant speed. Overshoot is RARE (5-25 % of entrances, 5-9 % when it
happens): give it to one hero pop per shot at most. Grouped elements stagger ~15 frames (0.5 s) apart, pills in a column
3-4 frames apart. Half the entrances carry a small sound on the frame of motion (slide = click, grow = boom/thump).

**Transitions (95 measured).** element moving inside a held shot 41 % (0.23 s), push 13 % (0.52 s), whip 7 % (0.53 s), dissolve
7 % (0.23 s), carry (an element rides over the cut into the next picture) 7 % (0.33 s), zoom into a thing 5 % (0.47 s), morph
5 % (0.26 s), white flash 4 % (0.23 s), plus hard cuts. Motion blur on 24 % of them, along the move. Pushes and whips SNAP
then settle (the same curve as entrances) and start ~0.15 s before the new sentence so the new picture is in on its word.
Direction: mostly vertical (down 19, up 12 of 46 directed moves; left 8, right 5). A whip flings the old hero up and out
with a little spin. Hook face/suspect swaps run every ~0.23 s, each new one snapping in.

**Life in the hold (65 holds).** The camera drifts 0 px a frame in 3 holds of 4; still, 98 % of frames have something moving:
about 9 % of the frame at once (p25-p75 4-17 %), at ~3 px a frame (2-5). What moves (vision pass on the 10 longest holds):
- ONE act per hold, on the word: a creature rising from the bottom edge on an arc (~1 s, ease-out, head turning to its
  target); a car arriving from off-frame (0.4-0.5 s, ~40 % of the remaining way per step early); a coin falling into a slot
  (gravity, 6-10 frames, then a new coin); an arm reaching toward another character (0.5-0.7 s, rotating about the shoulder);
  a heart thumping (3-5 % scale, double beat every 0.5-0.8 s); a gauge needle creeping and twitching (10-20 degrees, every
  0.3-0.6 s); a nucleus lighting up (0.4-0.7 s cross-change); HUD bars popping in one after another (~12 frames apart).
- Under it a quiet idle on the characters only: breathing 1-2 % of the torso at 1.5-2 s; head bob ~1 % of the frame height at
  ~1.8 s with a 1-2 degree tilt; hair following 1-4 frames behind the head; eye darts that SNAP to a new spot every 0.3-0.5 s
  (stepped, never sliding); blinks every 2-4 s (lids shut fast in ~2 frames, open slower in ~4); a talking character's mouth
  steps between shapes every 3-6 frames.
- A table or row of many items: EACH item its own small sine pulse (1-3 % scale, ~0.7 % of the frame bob, 0.6-1.2 s), every
  item on its own phase, so no two move together.
- Backgrounds, grounds, sets and settled labels never move. A built-up diagram may hold dead still once its parts land.
- Expression changes on the beat in ~8 frames (shock to worried), with sweat drops thrown off on the shot's entrance (~0.3 s).

**Label and diagram builds.** A date tag opens sideways from a thin sliver in 4-6 frames; the number plate under it wipes
on left to right in ~5-10 frames, 4 frames after the tag; a title pill types itself (~1.5 letters a frame) and widens with
the text; a cycle diagram's ring draws on in ~0.5 s (ease-out) with an arrowhead on its tip while its nodes pop in 2-4
frames apart, the main one overshooting once (~1.2x) and settling.

**Construction (close-up crops).**
- People: a tall U head in one flat skin colour, big half-disc ears a shade deeper, NO nose, no blush; dot eyes #2A0A4A with
  no catchlight in medium shots; close-ups get white eyes, a coloured iris and one catchlight, with skin-coloured lids that
  slide down for blinks and tired looks; muted capsule brows; a tiny dark-red capsule mouth (#A0142A). Hair is a dome with
  three flame-shaped locks; long hair falls behind the shoulders and flicks out into pointed tips. A visible deep-red neck
  with a chin shadow, a lighter rib trim on the collar, arms a touch lighter than the face, palms with four fingers and a thumb.
  Stylised skin runs from peach to deep brown-red (#F6B38A, #EE8A5A, #E8602F, #B9502E, #7A3A26, #5A2A1E).
- Talking objects: a coral lid over each eye, a pale blue shade (#CFEAFF) in the lower white, tall deep-blue pupils (#0D2F7A),
  white gloves shaded blue, red sneakers; on a backdrop they cast their whole silhouette.
- Organs: every organ is a soft mass with a darker edge band and a lighter inset copy of itself (a lobe of light inside).
  Lungs violet (#9A1ED8) with lighter veins; heart hot pink (#E03CC8) as lumps with a lighter lobe; stomach pink (#E46AE0);
  liver; gut as layered folds, each its own form with light stripes, food dots running through. The path the food takes is
  ringed in yellow (drawn as one edge pass under all the tract organs, then their fills). Bodies in diagrams are blue
  silhouettes with a thick deeper edge, overflowing the frame sides, head cropped by the top.

**Depth (every shot).** Far plane: the gradient ground, shapes hazed toward it and softly blurred, pale discs. Subject: crisp.
(§19: defocus is rare, ~1 shot in 8; atmospheric planes and big cut-off near shapes carry the depth.) Near plane: dark audience heads, big soft bokeh discs (cyan/magenta/yellow on dark scenes), washed-out blurred props close to
the lens. Heroes on a backdrop disc cast their whole silhouette onto it (down-right, the backdrop's darker tone). Places are
built in planes: far mountains pale and close to the sky colour, nearer hills darker, the ground darkest. Dark scenes glow
(radial glow, rays, bokeh); light scenes get stage beams or soft far discs.

**Small details that sell it.** An inset circle with a thick white ring and a short stalk pointing at what it explains; tape
banners tilted 3-8 degrees; fact pills bleeding off the left edge; titles in mixed sizes (a small line over a huge one),
tilted a few degrees; pie labels outside the pie; flat maps in two colours with small "~" wave marks on the sea and a soft
glow behind; tiny white specks on glossy forms; characters peeking in from a frame edge; clouds with faces and rain when the
topic is sad; a caveat plate at the top in navy.

## 15. Text, hooks, motion smoothness and sound (measured on 16 Shorts; wins over every earlier section)

### Hooks (16 hooks, frame by frame)
- The title is a **designed lockup**, never plain type: key words huge (cap ~150-180 px), filler words about a third of that, 2-3 colours,
  the key word in a vertical gradient, a dark ring right around the letters plus a light outer ring, usually a dark extrusion below-right,
  sometimes a puffy plate behind it, sometimes printed onto the hero object itself. Upper third, slightly tilted (-3 to 3 degrees).
- The hero is in the frame and ALREADY acting at frame 0 (chewing, pressing, lifting), overlapping the title.
- At ~2.4-2.8 s the title leaves as the nearest plane: it drops, zooms past the lens, flies up, or fades, while the hero stays and the camera
  carries on into shot 2. No hard cut out of the hook.

### Text
- Labels are SMALL and attached: pills, chevrons, pennants with a leader line and a dot on the thing, hexagon headings. Big type only for the
  hook and one key number. At most one tape banner per Short. Numbers sit on a tag or plate and count up.
- Data devices: cylinder bars, crowd fields that recede (far rows small and blurred), zoom bubbles on a stalk, node maps, icon tiles.

### Motion smoothness
- The newer references are **true 60 fps**: every frame is a new drawing while something moves (frame-repeat runs of 1 dominate; 35 % of frames
  are fully still, in holds). Older downloads at 30 fps hide this. Render flat Shorts at 60 fps; timings stay the same in seconds.
- Moves (348 measured at 60 fps with optical flow, deep5/ease60.json): median 24 frames at 60 fps (0.4 s; p25 0.2 s, p75 0.7 s).
  The speed builds softly and peaks at ~40 % of the move (p25 18 %, p75 66 %), then lands on a long soft tail; only a third start at
  full speed (the quick pops). This S-curve, not a snap that starts at full speed, is what reads as smooth. The engine's `glide`
  curve (slides, grows, MOVE, along, rings) and `popc` (pops) follow it; holds keep a quiet idle; ~35 % of frames are fully still.

### Sound (Demucs stems of 16 Shorts, every effect matched to its picture)
- **Music bed:** soft and slow, 52-85 bpm (median 70), mostly F major (8 of 16) or C major (5), sometimes A minor. Warm pads, gentle
  mallets or plucks, a round low end, **no drums** (percussive share 0.35). Spectrum: centroid ~1060 Hz, 40 % of energy under 250 Hz,
  8 % over 4 kHz. Sections swell ~11 dB (quiet pad-only stretches, fuller stretches). It sits **~22 dB under the voice while it speaks**
  and ~19 dB under in the pauses. Legion's own bed for this style: `flatkit/bed_cosmic.wav` (made in code by `flatkit/sound.py`, F major, 70 bpm).
- **Effects are rare:** 0.06-0.15 a second, so **3-6 in a 40 s Short**. 46 % sit inside words, 35 % land on a cut; every one has a picture
  partner (something appearing, a cut, a zoom). Kinds, by share:
  - **blip** 41 %: ~0.09 s soft sine tick falling about an octave, a faint click on top, centroid ~900 Hz. On a key thing appearing.
  - **pop** 33 %: ~0.07 s round pitched pop (a 2-semitone drop) over a soft low thump and a tiny click, centroid ~1300 Hz. On a pill, an icon, a character popping in.
  - **chime** 14 %: ~0.28 s soft mallet or bell pluck with a low body, no shimmer, centroid ~800 Hz. On a reveal or the answer.
  - **"whoosh"** 11 %: NOT air: a short dry falling thump-zip (~0.24 s, ~400 Hz down to the low end, crisp click). Half on cuts; on a big push or zoom.
  - click ~1 %: a dull wooden tap.
  Tuned, soft, dry, nothing over ~8 kHz. Never cartoon boings, record scratches, meme lines, real mouse clicks, glassy pings or long airy whooshes.
  Legion's own set for this style: `flatkit/sfx_cosmic/` (Blips, Pops, Chimes, Whooshes, Knocks; every pitch a note of F major).

## 16. Fourth study: characters, pace, hooks, every-frame motion, and the giveaways (10 Shorts at 60 fps; wins over everything above)
Measured with stylelab/pace60.py (deep6/pace60.json), stylelab/frame_motion.py (deep6/frame_motion.json, moves_*.jpg), word timings
for all 10 (deep6/*.words.json), full-resolution character frames (deep6/chars) and 0.25 s hook strips (deep6/hooks_strip.jpg).

### Characters
- People carry these Shorts: the "you" of the script is a person (a kid, a woman at a desk, a man with a moustache). Organs, molecules
  and objects get a face only when the script personifies them, for a shot or two (a brain on caffeine, a virus with a hair dryer,
  molecules with eyes). Personified things have big round white eyes with a dark pupil AND a catchlight, a tiny mouth, tiny limbs.
- Human head: a tall rounded U, half-disc ears, ONE deeper crescent down the shadow side of the face and neck, a small nose a shade
  deeper than the skin in most (a bulb for older men, sometimes none), thick short rounded brows, dark dot eyes without catchlights
  (white eyes with an iris only in close-ups). The MOOD IS IN THE LIDS: skin lids cut the dots flat across the top, slanting down to
  the outside when worried or sad, down to the inside when angry, level when tired; purple bags under tired or ill eyes; blush
  circles when hot, ill or embarrassed. Mouths are tiny: a capsule, an "o" with a pink tongue, a red zigzag when queasy or anxious.
- Hair: one big solid mass with ONE lighter same-hue band across the crown and a few flat strands or locks; fringes are cut shapes.
- Body: a simple torso about 1.45 heads wide, a contrasting collar ring, sleeves as separate shapes, thick tapering arms a touch
  lighter on the lit side, REAL fingers whenever a hand gestures (a hand on the chest, a fist under the chin), round shoes. Adults
  are about 4.5 heads tall; kids have bigger heads. Older men: moustache, a stubble zone in deeper skin, bulb nose.
- A fast limb move leaves two fading ghost copies for 2-3 frames (seen on a dizzy arm swing).

### Pace (against the voice)
- Hard cuts are far apart: median 5.75 s between them (p25 3.7, p75 8.6), about 16 words per shot. A shot EVOLVES instead of cutting:
  new elements arrive, the camera pushes or pans between subjects in one set, a line wipes a diagram to its next state, a zoom dives in.
- Inside a shot something new starts moving every 0.71 s (p25 0.47, p75 1.1): about every two words, ~1 motion a second.
- Each motion lands ON the word that names it: median +0.03 s after the word starts (p25 -0.08, p75 +0.10). Never early.

  numbers written as digits in about a quarter of them. The humour comes from the facts, never from quips.

### Hooks (10 Shorts, every 0.25 s)
- The first line spoken IS the title on screen, word for word, starting at 0.0 s and said in 1.5-2.8 s: a question ("How does [X]
  work?", "What actually is [X]?", "Can we [X]?", "What happens when you [X]?") or a bold claim or proposal ("Your [X] does [Y].",

- Frame 0 holds the title lockup, ONE hero (one object or one person, big, centred under the title) and a glowing gradient or dark
  ground. Nothing else: no sticker, no second character, no furniture.

  creature flies across). The title stays to 2.5-3.2 s (some grow in slightly over the first second) and leaves as the nearest plane.

### Every frame
- 60 fps; moves on the glide S-curve (speed peaks at ~40 %); ~1 travel in 4 and ~1 pop in 3 overshoot by ~12 % and settle ONCE;
  ~1 travel in 5 winds up ~10 % first; nothing ever wobbles.
- Rotation follows the path: cards and panels fly in turning and land on a 10-20 degree tilt. Squash and stretch stays subtle.
- Tags and plates open in two stages (a thin bar grows sideways, then grows to full height), their contents pop in after.
- The before and after of one diagram: a thin bright line sweeps across it (~0.45 s), the new state behind the line.
- Between two subjects in one set: a sideways pan, the planes moving at their own speeds.
- Reactions start 4-8 frames after their cause (the reaper arrives, THEN the woman tips back, pivoting at her feet).
- Dives into a planet or an organ: a zoom while the close surface fades in.

## 19. Seventh study: 20 more Shorts, depth, the cast, devices and why each element is there (study9; wins over everything above except section 20)
Measured on 20 new references (60 fps originals, 215 shots): contact sheets of every shot and 2 s strips of all 20 read by eye, 14
transcripts with word timings, full-resolution crops sampled for colour (CIELAB), a defocus map per frame (study9/depth9.json,
blur9.json), the three special transitions frame by frame (study9/full/strip_*.jpg) and the stylelab chains (measure/, timeline.md,
sound and motion logs). Rebuilt in code on output/flat_test/study9.html: the light-shaft surface, the fog graveyard, the space cast, a
gauge and a skull counter, a pixel assembly under a time skip, a split screen, dust-grain characters and the diagonal wipe.

### The register decides the cast (corrects §16 for these topics)
- **Space, what-if and animal topics:** personified things ARE the cast in about 15 of 20. Planets, stars, the Moon, a black hole,
  dust grains, asteroids and animals get faces and emotions and act out the facts across the whole Short. Faces are small and LOW on
  the body (`face()`), the mood carried by lids and brows; grains and rocks get white eyes with pupils.
- **A recurring host** appears in 3-6 shots, bookends the Short and stands in for "we" ("Let's...": the host tries the plan). Ours is
  always an ORIGINAL character made for the creator (never a known mascot): one simple body, one signature colour, one prop that fits
  the topic (a chef blob for "cooking a planet", a magician for a trick of physics).
- **Serious medical, social and war topics:** personification is rare; people, cells and objects carry it, as in §16-§18.
- **A gag is welcome when the joke ENCODES the fact** (measured): the slowest planet with a walker (slowest orbit, the number on a tag);
  a reversed card for winds that reverse; a cat in tangled yarn for a tangling rope; a bird in a trench coat for humans hiding in an
  animal ranking; a hockey mask for "the deadliest killer"; a ghost-faced gas cloud for an invisible killer gas. If the gag can be
  taken out without losing a fact, it goes (the §16 rule stays for gags for their own sake).
- **Speech bubbles:** short white rounded bubbles, black text, 1-4 words, said by a personified thing at its moment ("Okaay, let's go!",
  "Yes, really!", "You're welcome.", "Hypothetically!"); one bubble on screen at a time (`speech()`).

### Why an element is there (every element has ONE job; measured over all 215 shots)
- **Metaphor props, 3-6 per Short:** everyday objects that explain a mechanism in one picture: a cooking pot (making a planet), a bomb
  with a lit fuse (a threat with a timer), crime-scene tape and a wanted poster (the culprit), puppet strings (control), pause and play
  buttons (stopping a process), a progress or budget bar (how much is used), a newspaper (the news), a rubber stamp "ABANDONED" or
  "CERTIFIED" (a verdict), a calendar (time), a blueprint (a plan). Each lives for one sentence.
- **Data devices**, each tied to one spoken number: a tag with a thin leader line and a smaller sub-line (`tag()`); the skull counter
  (black plate, pale skull, white number: `skullTag()`); a grid of icons counting down (skulls going pale: `skull()`); a speedometer
  with zones and a digital readout (`gauge()`); a budget bar along the top; a line or bar chart with the host riding the line; a zoom
  bubble on a stalk (very frequent: the close-up of a thing inside the wide shot, `bubble()`); an isometric diorama tile for a place.
- **Comparisons:** a split screen with a white divider (two worlds), two stacked panels for "meanwhile", three strips for three states
  of one thing (`split()`).
- **Fine print:** a tiny caveat in a top corner ("Not to scale", "Enlarged for visual purposes", "Simplified") whenever sizes or
  distances are faked, and a source line at the bottom for a hard number (`fineprint()`). It keeps the picture honest.
- **Colour rhythm:** the base is a dark navy starfield (deep purple toward the bottom, round dots plus a few lavender 4-point sparkles,
  `twinkles()`); metaphor cutaways jump to full-bleed bright fields (cyan, yellow, magenta, purple) and back. The jump itself says
  "this is a comparison, not the real thing".

### Depth (measured; replaces the bokeh-first advice of §14)
- **Atmospheric planes carry the depth.** The far plane is pastel: its lightness is within ~10 of the sky and its chroma about half
  (far buildings L 79-92, chroma 29-31); near planes are saturated (chroma 67-82). Each step of a mass toward the viewer drops 5-8 L
  (a canopy: 54, 33, 31, 25). Use `landscape()` / `hills()` with these steps.
- **Dark scenes** (night, caves, graveyards): the MIDDLE plane is lit brightest (stones L 72), the near plane darker (L 42) with a lit
  rim along its top, far rows hazed into fog (L 25-60), the ground darkening toward the camera (L 29 to 21). Fog banks sit BETWEEN
  rows (`fog()`), drawn after a far row and before the next.
- **Big near shapes cut by the frame edge:** a tree canopy, tombstones, the backs of characters watching a screen, audience heads with a
  red rim light, the huge limb of a planet or the Moon in a bottom corner. Thickness comes from an extruded side (`extrude()`), never
  from a gradient.
- **Light:** god rays from ONE source off the frame (below), bloom halos on suns and stars, glow rings on black holes, rim lights on
  dark figures, drop-shadow ellipses under everything standing on a ground.
- **Perspective constructions** for space that is not a room: a neon grid floor (`perspGrid()`), a gravity funnel (the grid clipped to a
  funnel), a one-point aisle, a spiral tunnel, curved horizons (a planet surface is a huge disc with a rim line), isometric tiles.
- **Defocus blur is RARE:** about 1 shot in 8, and only on soft near props (a cloud, a leaf). Parallax of blurred to sharp planes is
  ~1.06 (p75 1.5): planes barely separate in speed. No grain (high-pass std 0.27); corners ~13 L darker than the centre (`vignette()`).

### Light shafts (one frame measured to the pixel: a planet surface at sunset)

### The space cast, built (`planet()`, `shard()`, `face()`)
- **Moon:** pale periwinkle, the shadow side ONE darker crescent, maria as darker blobs with a lighter inset rim, a ray crater (thin

### Transitions (three new ones, measured frame by frame)
- **Diagonal wipe** (`trans: { type: 'diag' }`): the new shot comes from the top behind an edge tilted ~16 degrees, higher on the right,
  snapping in and settling (0.6 s; dir 'up' brings it from the bottom). Used between two scenes of one argument.
- **Time skip** ("millions of years later", "let it bake"): a white three-arrow sign top left lit one arrow after another every 0.1 s,
  faint scanlines, glitch bars in cyan, blue, grey and white that thicken over ~3 s, the picture underneath stepping at 10 fps
  (`ffwd()` over a shot drawn on `FFT()` time).
- **Pixel assembly:** a thing builds out of square pixels (~1/50 of its size) flying in from scattered spots over ~4.5 s, then the
  smooth vector drawing takes over (`pixelIn()`). For "forming", "evolving", "being built".
- White flashes, split screens and whips as in §14.

### Hooks and scripts (14 transcripts)
- Hook lines are short and concrete; 6 of 14 open with "Let's [verb]...": a proposal the Short then carries out. Patterns: "Our [thing]
  has a silent killer.", "Our [thing] is boring. Let's [replace it].", "What happens if we [absurd act] all the [things]?", "Let's
  evolve, so you can [ability].", "Does [habit] make you [result]? Yes and no.", "Let's power a [machine] with [unlikely fuel].",
  "[Event] is upon us. What is it and why...", "[Threat] is back. What makes it so hard to stop?", "This is the world's oldest
  [surprising thing].", "Let's take 1 % of [resource]...", "There's a big dirty secret about [thing].".
- The title is a designed lockup over the hero, sometimes printed ON an object in the scene (a sign, a stamp, a billboard, a poster).
- **Four structures:** (1) the "Let's do X" thought experiment, step by step, each step with its consequence; (2) the tour of options:
  try A, its flaw, try B, its flaw..., back home; (3) the threat explainer: "silent killer", the mechanism, "And it gets worse.", what
  we do about it, a hopeful last line; (4) a question, "Yes and no.", both halves.
- Connectives that escalate: "But...", "And it gets worse.", "Sadly,". Pace 3.05 words a second (median of 14).

### How a flat shape is shaded (15,596 shadow pairs, 14,848 highlight pairs)
- The shade side is ~10 L darker AND turns hue. Per Lab hue band (30 red-pink, 90 yellow-orange, 150 green, 210 cyan, 270 blue, 330
  magenta): hue shift -8.5 / -11.4 / +5.6 / +13.4 / +8.6 / -0.6 degrees, dL 9.7 / 10.2 / 11.7 / 8.3 / 10.5 / 8.9, chroma x 0.95 /
  0.99 / 0.85 / 0.99 / 1.14 / 0.89. In words: yellows shade orange, greens and cyans shade bluer, blues shade violet, pinks shade
  toward magenta. On dark bases the shade loses chroma (x 0.85).
- The lit side is ~10 L lighter and turns the other way: +8.1 / +9.7 / -4.7 / -7.6 / -7.7 / +0.4 degrees (pinks light toward orange,
  yellows toward lemon, greens toward yellow-green, cyans and blues toward cyan). Dark bases gain chroma in the light (x 1.14); light
  bases lose it (x 0.83).
- In code: `shadeOf(col, k)` and `lightOf(col, k)` do exactly this. Never darken with plain black or lighten with plain white.
  `tone(col, dL, dh, kC)` moves a colour in LCh for anything else.

## 21. Density: how full a frame is, and when it goes sparse (all 3,868 frames measured; wins over everything above except sections 22-24)
Measured with `stylelab/density_measure.py` on every sampled frame of all 141 Shorts (caption band masked): flat shapes (colour-edge
segmentation, soft gradients ignored, anything over 0.02 % of the frame), small details (under 0.2 %), inner detail (edge share INSIDE
the drawn things, not the background), bald cells (grid cells of 6 x 10 with almost no edges). Study files: all/density/ (never shipped).
Our first Moon build looked "bald" next to the reference; the numbers say why: it sat around the reference's MEDIAN and never went
busy (max 48 shapes, 0 % of frames at 50+, the hook frame 23 shapes, inner detail 0.073).

### The numbers
- Shapes per frame: p10 9, p25 15, median 25, p75 42, p90 61. Small details: median 12 (p75 23, p90 38). Inner detail: median 0.081
  (p75 0.112, p90 0.147). Bald cells: median 33 % (p25 19 %).
- The mix: sparse (<12 shapes) 16 % of frames, normal (12-30) 42 %, busy (30-50) 25 %, very busy (50+) 17 %. FOUR frames in ten are busy.
- Every Short swings: its densest frame has ~85 shapes (median over 141 Shorts), its sparsest ~5. 94 % of Shorts have at least one 50+
  frame AND at least one frame under 12. Longest stretch with no busy (30+) frame: median 15 s (p75 22.5 s).
- The swing comes in steps: busy follows busy (18.5 % of neighbouring samples), normal follows normal (22.6 %); a jump from sparse
  straight to busy is rare (3.8 %).
- The top of the frame is not bald: edge share in the top quarter 0.031 (ours was 0.021). Clouds, stars, far planes, ceilings, hanging
  things, the lockup, tags.

### When a frame is busy, when it is sparse
- Frame 0 is the busiest frame of the Short: 44 shapes, inner detail 0.118, bald cells 21 %. The lockup sits over a FULL scene, not a
  hero on an empty gradient.
- Busy by place: city street 48, title card 46, building interior 39, forest 37, lab or hospital 34, landscape 32, home 32. By light: a
  lit scene 40, sunset 38. By cast: three characters 39, two 33, one 26, none 22. By job: hook 38, solution 33, example 31, history 30,
  bookend 29, consequence 28.
- Sparse by place: colour field 21 (bald 46 %), ocean 20, diagram 20, starfield 20, galaxy 19, solar-system diagram 15. By job: scale
  18, comparison 21, turn 22, number 22, mechanism 23. A sparse frame is still never empty: ONE big hero filling 30-60 % of the frame,
  often cut by an edge, plus one device; starfields stay dense in specks (inner detail 0.094).
- So: places, people and the hook are busy; ideas (a number, a mechanism, a scale, the turn) are sparse. The density follows the
  sentence's job, the same way the picture does (section 20).

### What fills a busy frame (looked at frame by frame; each filler belongs to the place, none is clutter)
1. Things repeated in perspective rows: a flock on a barn floor, a crowd, rows of plants, crops, corals, umbrellas, windows. Far rows
   small and close to the sky colour, near rows big and saturated, often ONE giant near copy cut by the bottom edge. `strew()`.
2. A dense near-plane tangle rising from the bottom edge: crops, reeds, weeds, roots, kelp. Broad leaves behind, thin crossing stems,
   pods or blossoms in one or two accent colours. Stems stay 15+ L from the leaves or it turns to mud. `tangle()`.
3. Tonal texture strokes on grounds: barn floors, sand, soil, crust, snow: short rounded strokes in the ground colour +/- 6-9 L.
   `dashes()`.
4. Radiating ribbons from the centre (a trip, an overload, a burst of signal): thick bands in two colours edged by a thin bright line,
   waving. `strands()`. Or a radial repeat of one object (machine arms, petals, satellites). `radial()`.
5. A motif on a garment, towel, wall or floor: hearts on shorts, stripes, dots, checks, tiles. `motif()`.
6. A device column on one edge (a list of foods, risks or symptoms as round chips with percentage pills). `edgeStack()`.
7. The architecture of an interior in the back plane: roof beams, a far door, hanging lamps, all within +/- 8 L of the wall so the cast
   stays the focus. `rafters()`, plus `room()` / `decor` / `furn()` (section 20).
8. Detail INSIDE the hero: panels, pipes, rivets and lights on a machine or a tech planet, windows on buildings, craters, shading
   crescents, patterns on clothes. Inner detail on busy frames sits at 0.10-0.15.
9. Specks, motes, bubbles, sparkles, sea glints, far boats, clouds and stars in the sky. `motes()`, `twinkles()`, `starfield()`,
   `cloud()`.

### Rules for every build
- Plan a density for each shot from its job: the hook frame VERY busy (40+ shapes: the lockup over a full scene); places, people,
  examples, solutions, history and the bookend busy (30-60); a number, mechanism, scale, turn or comparison may be sparse (10-25) with
  one big hero.
- Across the Short: at least 4 stills in 10 at 30+ shapes, at least one at 60+, no more than 2 in 10 under 12, and never more than
  ~15 s without a busy frame. Step between densities (busy, normal, sparse), don't jump straight from sparse to very busy.
- Fill with the fillers above. Each one must belong to the place and the sentence; never a random object, a gag prop or a sticker.
  Fillers live in the far plane (small, toward the sky colour) or the near plane (dark or saturated, cut by the frame); the hero stays
  the one brightest, most saturated, sharpest thing, and the caption band (0.70-0.84 H) stays calm.
- Heroes get inner detail (0.08+ inside them): shading crescents, panels, windows, patterns. A flat shape with nothing inside reads bald.
- The top quarter is never a bare gradient on a busy shot: clouds, stars, far planes, a ceiling, hanging lamps.
- `check.py` prints shapes, small details, inner detail and bald cells for every still and these Short-wide counts; fix the stills
  that miss before rendering.
- Checked on the Moon Short (output/density_moon_before_after.jpg): the same seven shots refilled with these tools went hook 18 -> 38,
  mangroves 48 -> 80, split screen 20 -> 38, reef 19 -> 66, bookend 30 -> 43.

  size. Cut a big hero (a planet, a machine, a building) by a side or the bottom edge in about 1 shot in 3 (`slot(job, { cut })`);
  the number shot keeps a smaller, whole hero.
- The title's centre in the upper quarter (~25 % of the height), about 2/3 of the width; the hero under it at mid-height.
- A device goes ON or right against what it explains (`against(box, 'on' | 'above' | 'side' | 'below', size)`), about a third of its
  size, tied by touching, a leader line or placement alone. Never floating away from it, never in the caption band.
- Three planes. Fill them with copies of a few kinds (rows, clusters), not more kinds: about 7 separate things a frame.
- A big near shape cut by the frame in about 1 shot in 3, at the bottom or a bottom corner, through two edges. Not in every shot.
- Overlap is how depth reads: near dressing may cross the hero's lower edge; people, hands and devices stay off its middle.
- Dress each place from the atlas above: its far, mid and near things at their usual heights.
- `check.py` prints placement (hero width, area, height, cuts; how far each device sits from the hero or cast it explains) next to
  these numbers; fix every FLAG before the critic. Mark heroes and devices you draw yourself (`mark()`) or the check can't see them.

## 23. Motion layers, timing, linked changes and the people, measured again (wins over everything above except section 24)
Measured with `stylelab/motion_layers.py` (dense optical flow on every 60 fps frame of 16 references, caption band masked; moves,
their peaks and their words), `stylelab/change_study.py` (205 idea changes in nine references, each named from a strip of frames)
and `stylelab/people_vision.py` (179 people, gridded, ratios in a fixed vocabulary). Study files: all/motion2/, all/motion3/,
all/changes/, all/people/ (never shipped). The dense Moon build through the same tools is "ours" below.

### Life: the picture never stands still
- Something moves in 94 % of frames (ours 75 %). In a typical frame ~12 % of the picture is moving (ours 4 %) at ~120 px/s at 1080
  wide (ours 80), in about 3 separate layers at different speeds (ours 2). 0-mover frames: 23 % (ours 54 %).
- What moves is WHOLE big shapes: bodies rock, breathe and swing their arms; the hero turns, bobs or sways; bubbles float; signs and
  text sway; clouds, dust and creatures drift behind. Not blinks and darting eyes alone.
- Engine: `alive(g, t, px, py, fn, { kind, size, seed })` gives any shape its loop ('bounce' standing, 'bob' floating, 'sway',
  'breathe', 'spin', 'drift'); `person()` now rocks on its feet (±1.8°, ~2.4 s), breathes (~1.2 s) and swings free arms (±6°) by
  itself. Every hero, cast member and big prop gets a loop; most shots get a drifting layer; seeds differ so nothing moves in step.

### Timing: one clear move per idea, its peak on the word
- A move's FASTEST frame lands on the word that names it: median 0.03 s before the word starts (p25 -0.11, p75 +0.06); so it starts
  ~0.1 s before the word (p25 -0.18, p75 +0.08). Ours started 0.19 s early (p75 -0.08: nearly every move early) because the builder
  subtracted hand-guessed leads (`w('x') - 0.2`, `- 0.35`).
- About 2.3 move onsets a second (ours 3.2). Moves last longer (median 0.3 s, p75 0.45 s; ours 0.2 / 0.32) with a gentler peak
  (median peak speed 27 vs ours 37) and land on a long soft tail; they come out of pictures that are already moving (a hit is ~16x
  the motion just before it; ours ~24x, out of dead stillness). Punch = the arrival exactly on the word, one clear move per idea, on
  a living picture; not more snaps.
- Engine: `hit(word, kind)` / `s.hit(word, kind, o)` (shot seconds) returns the start that puts that kind's peak on the word ('snap'
  1 frame, 'pop' 0.09 s, 'slide' 0.14 s, 'grow' 0.15 s, 'move' 0.53 d, 'along' 0.38 d). Never add or subtract a lead by hand.

### Linked changes: how one idea becomes the next
- 5.8 hard cuts a minute and 11.2 big changes a minute INSIDE a shot: two idea changes in three happen without a cut.
- The mix (205 changes): plain cut 24 %, rebuild in place 14 %, carry 13 %, pan to a new part of the same world 11 %, object wipe 7 %,
  zoom-out reveal 7 %, zoom-through 5 %, dissolve 4 %, match cut 4 %, push 3 %, whip 2 %, flash 1.5 %, morph 1 %.
- How they move: linked changes ~30 frames at 60 fps (0.5 s), fast in, soft out (rebuild, carry, wipe, reveal); pans 30-46 frames,
  even; a zoom-through ~21 frames; a whip ~16 frames with blur; a dissolve ~18 frames; a match cut is a hard cut where the same shape
  sits in the same place (a hero object and its label in the same layout, a thermometer that stays while the sky behind it swaps).
- What links them: the thing that stays (a cupcake, a car, a character, a ladder) while the background or the scene changes round it;
  the old scene turning out to sit in a bubble, a window, a polaroid or a pod of the new one (the camera pulls back); the camera
  diving into a bubble, a pod or a photo and coming out inside it; blue bars or a panel sweeping across with the hero staying centred;
  old pieces shrinking away while new ones arrive in the same frame.
- Engine: `trans` types 'carry' {x, y, over}, 'pan' {dir}, 'bars' {col, dir, from, n}, 'reveal' {x, y, r, shape, ring, glow, keep},
  'into' {x, y, r, peek}, 'match'; inside a shot `rebuild(s.t, t0, i, n)` and `morph`. Durations in TDUR follow the numbers above.

### People: the specific proportions
- ~3.9 heads tall (p25-p75 3.4-4.5; the old 'story' build was 5.4); legs 40 % of the height; shoulders 1.7 head widths; neck 0.4;
  arms 0.4 thick, tapering; hands half a head long.
- Head ROUND (w/h ~0.9; 70 % round). Eyes: dots 59 % (white eyes 27 %, mostly close-ups and big smiles), wide-set (centres 0.42 of
  the head apart), at the middle of the face (0.52 down from the top of the skull), 0.17 of the head wide. No nose 64 %. No mouth 45 %
  (60 % of full-body figures; a mouth comes with speech or a feeling: a small smile curve, a small 'o', a line). No brows 60 % (they
  come with a feeling: a thick bar or an angle). Ears small and round or none (54 % none).
- Torso: trapezoid with wide shoulders 34 %, tube 22 %, round 21 %, pear 17 % (full-body figures: pear 37 %); a plain rectangle 5 %.
- Shading: side two-tone 43 % (the half away from the light a step darker on the torso, the far arm and leg, the face), none 23 %,
  one crescent 21 %. Outlines 7 %.
- ~3 small details each: collar 39 %, neck shadow 38 %, fingers 36 %, blush 25 %, cuffs 23 %, ear inner 23 %, shoe soles 22 %,
  seams 20 %. Hair hugs the skull: a cap with a side swoop or fringe, a bob, a bun, a curly cap, a tuft or cowlick on top.
- They ACT: holding things, leaning on a desk, sitting in a chair, arms out wide, hands folded on the belly, pointing, three-quarter
  turns. Standing straight with the arms down is the exception.
- Engine: the default build 'kz' is this figure (the lineup re-measured with the same tool: 3.6-4.0 heads, head 0.9, eyes 0.17 /
  0.42 / 0.53, legs 0.40, shoulders 1.7); `o.form` 'trap' | 'pear' | 'round' | 'tube'; `POSE.*` acting poses; `hairStyle: 'swoop'`.

### Rules for every build
- Every hero, cast member and big prop has a life loop (`alive()`, person idle); most shots have a drifting layer behind.
- Every move starts at `s.hit(word, kind)`. One clear move per idea; no flurries of tiny snaps.
- Plan each idea change's type: about 1 in 4 a cut (some of them match cuts), the rest linked (rebuild, carry, pan, bars, reveal,
  into); at least one reveal or zoom-through in a Short of 8+ changes.
- People: the 'kz' build, a mix of torso forms, an acting pose from `POSE`, dot eyes, a mouth and brows only when they speak or
  feel, white eyes only in close-ups.
- `check.py` prints `life` (from l_ stills 0.1 s after each still) and `timing and transitions` (hand-guessed leads, the share of
  hard cuts): fix every FLAG before the critic.

## 24. WHY: every move, place, colour and camera has a reason in the words; and the small truths (wins over everything above)
Measured with `stylelab/why_deep.py` on 201 shots of 36 Shorts (1,027 moves): per shot its first and last frames, the frames at its two
biggest motion peaks, a heat map of everything that moved, and the words spoken in it; Legion's brain named the reason for every move,
the place, the colour and the camera in a fixed vocabulary and wrote the rule each shot follows (study files all/why2/, never shipped).

### The numbers
- ~5 separate moves a shot; 89 % of them land on a spoken word. About 45 % of the frame stays still through a shot (the rest lives).
- Why things move: keeps it alive 19 %, stresses a key word or number 16 %, acts the word out 15 %, changes the idea 12 %, shows the
  mechanism 12 %, points to what comes next 8 %, shows scale or context 8 %, shows a feeling 7 %, a joke beat 3 %.
- Why the place: where it happens 30 %, an abstract field for a number or idea 27 %, a metaphor for the idea 18 %, inside the body or
  object 10 %, contrast with the last shot 9 %, the character's home base 6 %.
- Why the colour: the channel's palette 28 %, contrast with the last shot 26 %, the mood of the line 23 %, the subject's real colour 11 %,
  danger or warning 10 %.
- Camera: still 46 % (held for reading 42 %), push-in 19 % (emphasis 21 %), cut-in 9 %, zoom-through 7 %, pan 7 %, pull-out 6 %.
- Why an element is there: inform 37 %, a character 16 %, depth 16 %, place 11 %, dressing 9 %, guide the eye 7 %, joke 5 %.

### Motion: why something moves, and why the rest holds still
- When a line names a feeling, a physical action or a verb, have the characters act it out on that exact word (lean, bump, flinch, grin, sweep) and let the title and background hold still, because about 90% of moves land on a spoken word and the viewer reads the word and the picture as one thing (acts-the-word ~15%, mentioned in a dozen rules).
- When the line stresses a number, a name or a payoff word, give that word one distinct beat: a push-in, a scale-up with a burst of rays, a counter ticking up, a needle jumping or a badge popping in, because the key word needs a visible spike while the rest of the shot stays calm (~16% of moves).
- When a line explains a mechanism or process, show one object travelling a fixed track or changing step by step, one move per step word, while the scene around it holds, because the eye follows the single moving thing and the cause-and-effect reads cleanly (~12% of moves; the most repeated rule).
- When the line gives a cause and then an effect, fire the trigger on the cause word and let the effect bloom a beat later on the effect word, with the frame locked, because the delay lets the viewer see the link.
- When the line lists items, make each item pop in on its own word at a fixed spot (or as stacked cards) on a quiet background, because the list then builds beat by beat and stays readable.
- When the line turns, with a 'but', a question, a new claim or a 'now', swap the picture on that pivot word and sometimes turn the pivot word into giant title text, because the viewer should feel the change of idea at the instant they hear it (changes-the-idea ~12%, most long-shot rules say a new picture per sentence).
- When a shot has no key action, still keep small ambient loops going (blink, bob, flicker, drift, bubbles rising, a sway of 1-3%), because about a fifth of all moves exist only to stop a held image from freezing, and a median of ~45% of a shot is otherwise still.
- When a line is a joke or a punchline, let a character react with a one-word speech bubble, a face change or a prop, or pay it off with a loud burst and then empty the frame, because the joke needs its own beat and the silence after it makes it land (~3% of moves, in many rules).
- When a line is short or text-heavy (a title, a question, a number card), hold the background and camera still and let only one new thing arrive on the first word, because the viewer needs two seconds to read it.

### Place
- When the line is abstract, a number, a count or a statistic, move the action to a plain abstract field (flat gradient, dark void, dots, a chart) and put the number label on its word, because a clean field makes the number readable (~27% of shots).
- When the line is an analogy or a hard idea, show the everyday object first, then swap it for the real thing on the linking word and keep it moving through the payoff, because the metaphor turns the idea into something physical (~18% of shots).
- When the line goes inside a body, machine or cell, cut or dive to a cutaway of that interior and keep the cutaway locked while a colour fill or particles show the action, because a literal view of the mechanism beats describing it (~10% of shots).
- When the line places an event somewhere (a country, a room, a planet), draw it as a recognisable setting with a label that arrives on its name, because the place anchors the claim (~30% of shots are set 'where it happens').
- When the idea changes, make the new setting clearly different from the last one (a hard cut or a wipe on the key word), or keep the same characters in a new world, because contrast between shots tells the viewer the topic has moved (~9% of shots).

### Colour and light
- When a new idea or a new question starts, change the background colour (often by a wipe) on the first word, because about a quarter of colour choices exist to contrast with the previous shot and so mark the change.
- When the line carries a mood (cold and sad, warm and hopeful, sickly, dreamy), set the whole scene's colour to that mood on that word, and darken it on loss or an ending, because colour carries the feeling (~23% of shots).
- When the line names a danger, a threat or a warning, flood the scene or the threat's own shapes in red or a hazard colour, with a sign or a health bar on the key word, because the warning should land before the sentence ends (~10% of shots).
- When nothing in the line demands a special colour, draw in the channel's fixed palette and keep one accent colour for what matters, and use a thing's real colour only when it helps to recognise it, because a stable palette makes the channel recognisable (~28% palette, ~11% real colour).

### Camera
- When the viewer must read text, a diagram or a list, hold the camera still, because about 42% of shots are plain holds for reading, and the shot also uses a slow push to stay alive.
- When a word needs emphasis (a number, a punchline, a heavy question), push the camera in toward that thing, ending on a face for jokes or heavy questions, because the push tells the viewer where to look (~21% of shots).
- When the line goes inside, down or into a bigger scale, dive or plunge the camera through the surface or layers to the new scale, and pull back out when the line moves to a mass or a system, because the camera move shows the change in size.
- When a shot has to hand over to the next, use a wipe, a fast blurred whip, a pan or foreground shapes closing the frame, because the cut then feels like part of the story (~9% of shots).

### Elements
- When a line carries information, make most elements carry it (labels, numbers, gauges, cards, titles) and have them arrive on their words, because informing is the most common job of an element (~37%).
- When a character appears, give it a job: a stand-in for the viewer who reacts, a host who holds still in the middle, or a guide who enters at a new idea with a question bubble, because characters make up ~16% of elements and each has a role.
- When a scene is filled in, add foreground and background layers that drift at different speeds, plus small dressing (stickers, sparkles, clouds) at the edges, because depth and dressing (~25% of elements) keep a flat picture alive without taking the focus.
- When the eye must be led, add an arrow, a spotlight, a zoom circle, a scan sweep or speed lines to the one thing that matters, because guide-the-eye elements (~7%) keep one focal point per frame.

### Never
- Never move the whole frame at once: if the text or the idea needs reading, the background and camera hold still and only one thing moves.
- Never leave a held picture completely frozen: even a still shot gets a small blink, drift or flicker.
- Never make a move without a word to hang it on: moves land on a spoken word, not at random times in a sentence.
- Never use one background or colour for two different ideas in a row: each new idea changes the set or colour.
- Never use a long, plain voiceover over one unchanging picture, or crowd a shot with several things competing: each new claim gets its own picture and one focal point.

### The small truths (from the creator's notes on the sleep Short; each one is a rule now)
- Everything that stands, stands ON something: furniture, beds, people and animals have their bottom on or below the floor line, with
  a floor shadow (a bed hanging on the wall reads as "everything floats"). `check.py` prints `grounding`.
- Sleep is shown the reference's two ways: a small bed in the 30-degree view standing on the floor with the sleeper tucked in, head on the
  pillow, 'z's floating up (`isoBed`), or the close-up from above, head on a big pillow, the blanket to the chin, both hands over the
  fold (`bedTop`). Never a standing figure turned on its side, never someone standing on top of a mattress.
- Anything that runs, runs: a rat on a turning wheel runs, its gait driven by the wheel (`critter` pose 'run', `phase`); a wheel turning
  under a still animal reads as broken. The reference's lab mouse / rat: a long egg head with a pointed snout and a big pink nose ball,
  big round ears with pink insides, white eyes, slim legs, a long thin pink tail (never a round bear face).
- An arm is one unbroken piece from shoulder to hand: a hand that touches or follows something uses `person({ reachR: [x, y] })`; an
  arm in front of a desk is the same person drawn again with `only: 'R'` after the desk. A separately drawn arm splits at the shoulder.

### Rules for every build
- In NOTES.md, every planned move carries its reason tag (acts-the-word, stresses, mechanism, alive, changes-the-idea, points, scale,
  feeling, joke), and every shot its place reason, colour reason and camera reason from the lists above. A move with no reason is cut;
  a shot with no idle loop gets one.
- The key word of each sentence gets ONE distinct beat (a push-in, a pop with rays, a counter, a needle jump) while the rest holds.
- Each new idea changes the set or the colour on its first word; a number goes on a plain field; a mechanism is one object moving along
  a track, one step per step word; a cause fires on the cause word and its effect blooms on the effect word.

## 25. The creator's quality bar (2026-10-27; wins over EVERYTHING above, sections 1-24 included)
The goal is pro-animator quality, the version that ends "not even close" comments. Every point below is checked; the engine does
the parts marked AUTO by itself, the builder owns the rest.

**1. Motion system (the biggest one)**
- 10-15 % of the frame is gently moving almost all the time (check.py `life`: median 10 % or more, at most 1 hold in 6 under 5 %).
- Every major character and key prop has a continuous low-amplitude idle that never fully stops: `person` idle (breathe ~1.2 s,
  rock ±1.8°, arm swing with the forearm lagging) and `alive(g, t, ...)` on every hero and big prop (bounce / bob / sway / breathe),
  seeds staggered so neighbours never move in step. Background pieces drift or bob (`alive` 'drift' / 'bob', motes, clouds).
- Layered motion on every action: primary (the body) + secondary (the arm follows 2-4 frames later) + tertiary (the hand, hair,
  a held object lags and settles 4-8 frames after). Overlap and follow-through on limbs, clothing, hair, held things.
- Ease in and out on almost everything (`glide`, `io`, ENTER kinds); linear only for a constant drift; snaps only on purpose.
- Weight: a heavy thing starts slow and stops slow (0.5-0.7 s, small overshoot); a light thing bounces more (overshoot 8-12 %).
  AUTO with `motion.weight`: `alive()` works the weight out from `o.size` (a 900 px whale loops slower and smaller, a 120 px bird
  quicker and springier). On moves give `mass`: `MOVE(t, t0, a, b, { d, mass: 2 })` for a planet, a whale, a falling crate (slow
  start, longer, late landing), `mass: 0.5` for a coin, a bird, a spark (jumps off, overshoots ~10 %, settles once). `s.hit(word,
  'move', { d, mass })` keeps the fastest frame on the word with the longer move.
- AUTO: every shot's camera breathes (job.json `motion.cam: "drift"`: a slow push 2 % to 4 % plus a small drift), so `depth()`
  layers and `bg()` get parallax for free. A shot that must be locked off says `{ lock: true }` (rare, on purpose).

**2. Character design and construction**
- 3.7-4.0 heads tall (engine `kz` build: ~3.8), wide heavy shoulders (sh 1.84 head widths), shorter legs (~37 %), the centre of
  gravity low: they look grounded.
- Pure dot eyes, almost never a nose, a mouth only while speaking (`talk`) or emoting, brows only when the mood needs them.
- One side of the body a consistent shade darker (`tone`, `light`), the same light direction for every figure in the Short.
- Hands and feet with weight: bigger, simple, readable shapes (hand 1.25, shoe 0.29); feet planted on the floor line (grounding).
- Poses are intentional and weighted: knees bend before a jump, the body leans into a push, arms rest, never floaty.

**3. Transitions**
- Near-zero hard cuts (check.py flags more than 15 % of idea changes as plain cuts). AUTO: a shot that gives no `trans` gets the
  style's linked change in turn (job.json `motion.trans`: pan, carry, zoom, pan, reveal, bars).
- Better than the default: a designed link with a motive, where something on screen leads the eye: the camera pushes through an
  object (`into`), an object shrinks into the next scene (`reveal`), one continuous camera move (`pan`), a morph (`morph()`), a
  content wipe (`bars`), a match cut (`match`: the same shape in the same place), the thing that stays (`carry` with its x, y).
- Smooth easing and readable timing on every transition (the TDUR / LEAD table already lands the fast part on the word).

**4. Timing and staging**
- The biggest visual hits land exactly on the stressed word or syllable: start every move with `s.hit(word, kind)`, never a
  hand-guessed lead. AUTO with `motion.syllable`: Legion measures the voice and every word carries how hard it is stressed and
  when its loudest syllable starts, so `hit()` lands the peak on that syllable ("eNORmous" pops on "NOR"). `stressed(from, to)`
  lists the words the voice leans on in a stretch, loudest first: the shot's BIG move (the planet arriving, the zoom, the slam) goes
  on the top one; small moves go on the other words. One clear primary idea per moment; secondary motions slightly delayed and eased; no competing motions.
- Still holds are extremely rare and intentional. Anticipation before big moves (a 3-6 frame wind-up the other way).
- Clean staging: the important action is readable and centred or properly composed (check.py composition and placement).

**5. Illustration quality**
- Shapes clean but not perfectly geometric (a little wobble in big outlines, rounded corners of different radii).
- More small purposeful details in the background and mid-ground, never clutter (density section 21 numbers).
- Intentional soft gradients and one consistent shadow language for the whole Short; the palette stays in the style's range,
  few contrast spikes.

**6. Camera**: almost never locked off (AUTO drift above); gentle pushes, light parallax between layers, every big camera move
eased and motivated by the words.

**7. Polish and consistency**
- No accidental still frames or frozen moments (life check). Particles, labels, arrows and callouts follow the same easing and
  timing rules as the main animation (they enter with ENTER kinds on their word and leave eased).
- Sound design hits on the exact frame of the visual event, not just near the voice: after the render, `python sync_check.py
  --fix` snaps every cue that is 1-5 frames off onto its measured visual hit and lists sounds with no visual event.
- Overall pacing matches the style's Shorts rhythm: not slow, not twitchy (one main move per idea, about 2.3 moves a second).

**8. The creator's second pass (2026-10-27, after the first test Short): "the alive feeling is still missing"**
- Characters were frozen while the text moved, and poses switched on one frame. Now: every character's acting is a key-pose list
  through `act()` (overlap: body, then shoulders, elbows, wrists, head; follow-through: overshoot and settle; anticipation on big
  moves); AUTO with `motion.life`: a bigger idle on every person and bust (breath, rock, sway, arms trailing), close-ups included.
- Backgrounds were locked. AUTO with `motion.life`: `depth()` planes drift on their own and every shot has floating motes in three
  depths; the builder still adds the shot's own environmental motion (steam, clocks, leaves, lights) on top.
- Changes read as slides. At most 3 in 10 slides; `into` / `zoom` with no x, y go through the old shot's hero by themselves; pans
  travel with a slight push-in and smear. The style's automatic list is carry, pan, into, reveal, carry, zoom, bars, pan.
