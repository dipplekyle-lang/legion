# Style study: satellite-map explainer Shorts (six references, measured)

> Six reference Shorts, called ref-A to ref-F. All 1080x1920, 30 fps, 64-105 s. Every number comes from the study tools
> (`measure/`, `deep/`, `deep3/`, `scenes/`, `hooks/`, `why/`, `env/`, `timeline.md`) and from looking at the frames.
> Learn the craft only; never copy a channel's scripts, titles, names, icons or pictures.

## 1. What the style is
A faceless geography explainer. The whole Short happens on ONE continuous satellite world map (real-looking terrain,
deep teal ocean, muted greens and sand). The place the sentence is about is lit up as a flat colour fill (orange, red,
yellow, green, blue, purple) on that muted map, a small flat icon or label sits on it, and the camera glides, zooms
or flies to the next place. There are no characters acting, no sets, no outlines: the map is the stage, the camera is the
actor, and the narrator's words appear as captions at the bottom. Every noun in the voice gets a picture on the map the
moment it is said. Look: photographic base, flat graphic overlays. Mood: clear, curious, "did you know".
Why it works: the viewer always knows WHERE a thing is, one thing at a time, and the map gives a free sense of scale.

## 2. Script formula
- **Hook sentence 13-32 words (median 16), 4.0-8.2 s.** Shapes: "Did you know [country/thing] [absurd claim]?" (ref-B, C, D: 3 of 6),
  "This [person] spent [huge number] [extreme act] with just one rule" (ref-A), "What if [big change] today?" (ref-F),
  "You ever wonder why [familiar thing is odd]?" (ref-E). The first sentence always holds one strange fact, one number or scale,
  and ends on an open question ("which country?", "what was the rule?"). Understood by 1-2 s.
- Narration starts at 0.0 s in every video. No intro, no title card, no "welcome".
- **Words per second 2.8-3.6** (median 3.2): clear, not rushed. Sentences 8-20 words, plain words, numbers spelled as they are
  said ("28 years", "1.5 billion", "31 days").
- Structure of a 65-80 s Short: hook (0-6 s), the setup in 2-3 facts (6-25 s), the mechanism or the journey as a chain of
  places, each with a number (25-60 s), the turn (a dead end, a catch, a surprising reason) at about 70-80 % of the length,
  and the ending: either the named answer ("...Qatar.", the country revealed only in the last sentence, ref-B) or a final
  place ("Hull, England", the route ends where it began, ref-A) or a call to comment ("decide below", ref-F).
- Each sentence = one place or one number. A sentence that needs two maps is split.
- Why: a place per sentence is what lets the camera move every 1-3 s without ever being decoration.

## 3. Voice and sound
- One narrator, warm mid voice (fundamental about 147-182 Hz: 4 of 6 between 147 and 161 Hz), no accent games, loudness -15 LUFS integrated, loudness range
  1.8 LU (very even), true peak about -0.2 dBFS. Gaps between words are almost zero (median 0.0 s, 88 % under 60 ms): continuous reading.
- Real pauses: 0.28-0.56 s, only 5-14 per Short, always after a comma or a full stop before a turn ("...a dead end." pause "Then...").
  The longest pause (0.5 s) sits right before the final line.
- **Music bed 8-14 dB under the voice** (bed about -30 dBFS against voice -16), centroid 690-1100 Hz, 19-39 % of its energy below 250 Hz.
  Light, steady, no melody that competes. It dips for pauses (9-19 measured per Short).
- **Sound hits 1.3-1.9 per second** (93-163 per Short). Kinds: booms (about 60 %), clicks (about 25 %), thumps (about 8 %), impacts (rare).
  **92-99 % of hits land inside spoken words, only 0-3 % land within 80 ms of a camera cut.** So a hit marks a word or a thing appearing
  (an icon pops in, a label slides in, a fill lights up), never the camera move itself. Each hit is short and dry.
- Why: the sound is the "pop" of each graphic arriving, tied to the word that caused it.

## 4. Pacing
- Shots (a stretch with one anchor on screen): **median 2.4 s, p90 9.3 s**, min 0.4 s, max 25 s (one long hold on a single map, ref-F, while
  graphics come and go over it). Per reference median 2.1-2.8 s.
- **Hard cuts: 0-3 per video.** Place changes are camera moves (fly, zoom, pan), 11-29 per video, **0.13-1.5 s each** (most 0.3-0.7 s).
- Quick dissolves: 0-5 per video, 0.13-0.7 s, used to jump to a very different scale (e.g. a flat map to a close terrain), never to black or white.
- In the first 3 s: 1-2 camera moves, 8-14 words. The first shot is 0.7-1.1 s in 4 of 6 videos (a fast reveal) or a 3 s tight satellite close-up
  that pulls back (ref-A).
- Near-still frames 9-52 %; median motion 0.9-5.3 (0-255 scale). When the camera rests, graphics still move (a route draws, a number counts, an icon pops).
  Never a frame where nothing at all changes for more than about 1 s.
- Why: the viewer's eye is paid with a new map fact every 2-3 s; the pace is set by the words, not by a cut grid.

## 5. Camera
- The camera is a top-down satellite view, **flat lens, no perspective, no depth of field**. Moves are zoom, pan and the flights that combine
  both. Roll is tiny (rotation p95 0.3-1.2 deg per frame) and used only in "fly along a path" moments.
- Speed profile: median 0.0-0.4 % of frame width per frame (holds), p90 0.5-4.8, p99 1.9-12 %. A move accelerates and decelerates smoothly
  (peak at 33-69 % of the move: ease in and out), no overshoot, no shake (jitter under 1 % of width).
- Hold: slow drift of 0-0.2 % of width per frame; 14-36 % of holds push slowly in or out. A fully dead hold is allowed only in ref-D style
  (road network drawn on a fixed map).
- Zoom ranges: world view (continents visible, Greenland and Africa both in frame) to street-scale terrain (a district fills the frame). A
  Short uses 3-4 zoom levels; it zooms in to a place being explained and out to relate it to the world.
- A **route camera**: when a journey is told, the camera travels along the drawn line at walking-story pace and the line grows under it
  (ref-A, E). The line is always ahead of or under the centre, never behind.
- Why: zoom out = "how big", zoom in = "right here", fly = "and then he went to".

## 6. Composition and framing
- Subject sits **centre of the upper two thirds (face/pin centre y about 0.35-0.5 of height)**. The lower third (y 0.62-0.78) is the caption band.
- One anchor per frame: a filled country, a route, a pin, or a ring. Graphics around it are small (icons 8-14 % of width) and 1-5 per frame.
- Foreground = graphics layer (labels, icons, route, rings); background = map. The map is darkened slightly (brightness 0.37-0.50 on a 0-1
  scale, saturation 0.28-0.77) so that coloured fills read at a glance: **fill saturation is higher than the map's, always.**
- Frame busyness: median 1 colour region of graphics, edge density about 0.087, about 310 distinct colours (photographic base, few graphic regions). Cleanliness is on purpose.
- Occasional dead-space framing: the world map shows white polar ice and a blue grid tile (a flat "table" edge) at the top/bottom of the frame
  in zoomed-out shots; it is the map's edge and a signature of the map layer (see 7).
- Why: the highlighted place is the only saturated area, so the eye never searches.

## 7. The look
- **Base map**: satellite imagery, true-colour. Ocean deep teal-navy (about #0F3A4D to #17566B), forests dark green (#2F4A2A to #4C6B34), sand/desert
  tan (#B89B6A to #D2B27C), ice white (#F1F4F6). Soft sun shading baked in, no labels on the base. Looks real; fills do not.
- **Highlight fills** (flat colour, hard edge following the real border, about 85-95 % opaque so terrain shows faintly): orange #F59A23, red #D93A32,
  yellow #F2D22E, green #3BBE5A, blue #3C9CEB, purple #8E3FB7. One colour per idea: the subject is warm (orange/yellow/red); obstacles red; allowed/ok
  green; water/foreign neutral cyan. Max 3 fill colours in a frame.
- **Route line**: yellow #F6D80A, 6-10 px wide, rounded ends, slight curve, draws on at walking pace; a yellow dot marks the current end. Dashed white
  arrow for "a plan/intention" (ref-A), dashed red line for a boundary/axis (ref-E).
- **Cyan glow** (#2BD0E8) is allowed ONLY as a thin outline on continents when a "whole world" network is shown (ref-E); no other glow.
- **Labels**: red banner #E0372D with white bold sans text, tilted 3-8 degrees, for a headline fact; small white pill label with black text for a place name;
  yellow-on-black bar for a headline overlay. Corners hard, a tiny drop shadow only.
- **Icons**: flat vector, one or two colours, a thick white or light outline for contrast (e.g. cars/planes/ships in red hexagon plates, a document, a phone held by a
  hand, a pin, a jail door, a ring). Flat and slightly isometric buildings are allowed. A few emoji faces (shocked, thinking) mark a reaction.
- **Inserts**: a torn-paper news clip card on top of the map (headline and one highlighted line) to prove a claim (ref-F); a framed vintage paper map as the last
  reveal (ref-E); a photo in a hexagon badge for a real person (ref-A). Inserts slide in within 0.3 s, sit 2-4 s, leave.
- Grade: slight vignette (centre brighter than edges, about 0.88 edge ratio), faint grain (about 7 on 255). Contrast medium; never a colour wash.
- Why: a flat saturated fill on a muted photographic map is the cheapest way to say "this place" without a pointer.

## 8. Characters
There are no recurring characters. People appear only as: (a) a face in a **hexagon badge** (about 17 % of width) that sits on the map and moves with the camera for a person's story;
(b) small flat white figures/pictograms standing on the map to count people (5 per frame at most); (c) a flat illustrated person with a thought pose, partly cropped at the
bottom edge, for the closing question (ref-F); (d) a bride-and-groom or hands-and-keys flat pair for a concrete act. 96 % of frames have no head. They are always
flat, never animated beyond a pop-in and a slight bob. They stand ON the map (the place is their floor); they never float over the caption band.

## 9. Sets and props
No sets. The "set" is the map layer: continents, a zoomed terrain, a district, a coastline. Props are graphics: pins, rings (concentric circles pulsing once for "right here"),
grids (the blue tile at the map edge), route lines, plates with icons, hands, signs ("FOR RENT" style), cards. Recurring motifs: ring around the key spot, route line, number
tag next to the line, country name set in huge bold white letters ON its own fill (ref-A Kazakhstan, ref-F Africa), a thin white arrow pointing from a label to its target.
Dressing density: sparse. Median of 3-5 assets per shot, each with a job (place, frame, inform, dressing); "dressing" only the ocean.

## 10. On-screen text and captions
- **Captions (always on, 78-99 % of the time)**: white bold geometric sans, about 24-34 px glyph height at 1080 wide, centred at x 0.50, **y centre about 0.68-0.73 of the height**, black
  soft shadow/outline, 2-6 words at a time (width median 31 %, p90 53 % of the frame), changing 5.7-8.8 times per second. Caption changes land on the word start (median delta 0.0 s).
  The key word of the phrase (a place, a number) is yellow #F6D80A (or green/red when it carries meaning).
- **Headline overlays** (hook and big claims): up to 2 lines, bold white with the key word in red or green, on a black bar, at y 0.28-0.35, typed on word by word as the voice says it
  (about 0.25 s a word), up to about 60 characters. Replaced by the next one rather than stacked.
- **Number tags**: "28 years", "2 years", "11 years", "31 days", "25%", "1.5 Billion": tilted bold white with a coloured plate, or huge white text with a dark glow, placed beside the thing it measures, count-up for money/people.
- **Place names**: white text with a thin dark outline, about 4 % of width tall for towns, 10-15 % for countries on their fill.
- Text sits on a plain area (ocean or flat fill), never over a face, never over the caption band (headline overlays are above it).
- Why: two text layers with different jobs (what is being said, what it is about) keep a Short that is watched on mute understandable.

## 11. The hook (first 2 s, frame by frame)
- 0.00-0.25: a tight or empty map; the first caption is already running; the highlight is already there (a colour blob, a close terrain) with no label yet.
- 0.25-0.75: **the first noun-graphic lands** (badge, country fill, ring), with its sound hit. The headline types on from 0.5-1.0 s.
- 0.75-2.0: the camera pulls back or the fill grows; a second graphic (a number plate, a couple, a for-rent sign) arrives at the word that names it (about 1.5-2.7 s).
- By 1-2 s the viewer knows the odd fact and holds the open question ("which country?"). Strange element in the picture: a single colour on an otherwise muted map.
- Hook pattern types seen: strange act in progress (ref-A), claim about a country (ref-B, C), hidden secret about a network (ref-D), "why is the map like this" (ref-E), what-if (ref-F).
- 5-6 camera moves are NOT needed: 1-2 moves in the first 3 s, 8-14 words.

## 12. KEEP vs CHANGE
**KEEP (identical):** one continuous satellite map as the stage; one anchor per frame; a graphic lands on the word it names; the fill-on-muted-map contrast;
yellow route line with the end dot; red tilted headline plate; 2-6 word captions with the key word yellow at y about 0.70; camera never cuts but flies (0.3-0.7 s ease in/out);
2.4 s median per shot; 3.2 words/s narration, almost no gaps; music 8-14 dB under the voice; 1.3-1.9 sound hits per second inside words, not on cuts;
the reveal placed in the last sentence; quick-dissolve only for scale jumps.
**CHANGE (theirs):** the topic, the places, the words, the order of facts, the exact icons and plate designs, the narrator voice, the channel name, the accent colour of the headline plate (one
accent per channel), the badge portrait, the final question.

## 13. DON'T
- No random gradients, no glowing text (only the one cyan network outline allowed), no particle bursts, no everything-fades-in, no fake app UI, no two things fighting for attention.
- No black/white fades. No hard cuts between places (fly or dissolve at a scale jump).
- No characters walking, talking, lip-syncing or filling the frame; no 3D perspective on the map; no depth of field.
- No map labels baked into the base map competing with captions; no borders drawn on every country at once (only the ones the sentence names).
- No more than 3 fill colours in a frame; no fill whose border does not follow the real coastline.
- No static hold longer than about 1 s without a graphic moving; no caption under 2 words or over 6.
- No long stretch (over 25 s) of a single zoom level.

## 14. WHY (the reason behind each choice)
- Fill colour on place (about 45 % of shots, "explanation"): shows WHERE; the colour carries the meaning (warm = subject, red = obstacle/problem, green = fine/allowed).
- Route line growing with the camera (journey stories): acts the verb "walked/travelled"; the camera speed equals the story speed.
- Icon on the place (about 35 %): illustrates a noun the narrator just said so the picture is the word (a plane, a phone, a jail, a couple).
- Number tag (about 20 %): stresses a number at the exact word; tilts and pops so the eye lands on it, then it gets out of the way.
- Zoom out (about 15 % of moves): changes the idea to "how big / how it compares"; zoom in (about 25 %): "right here, this detail".
- Dissolve (about 4 % of transitions, 0.13-0.7 s): jumps scale (world to street) without a visible cut.
- Insert card (about 6 %): proof for a claim, shown as a document so it looks sourced.
- Emoji face (about 5 %): the narrator's reaction to a shocking number, one at a time.
- Hold with a drifting camera: keeps a long fact alive for 3-9 s while captions and graphics change.
- A move with no reason (cut it): the camera moves while the words stay on the same place; a graphic that is not named in the sentence; a second highlight that is not part of the point.
- Label shares per sentence role (from `why/`): explanation 43 %, consequence/shock 16 %, waiting/tension 16 %, hook 6 %, punchline 6 %. Moves per sentence: hook 2.2, shock 1.4, explanation 0.8, tension 0.4, punchline 0.5 (the end is calmer).
- Brightness and saturation barely change with mood (correlation with valence under 0.35): the grade stays the same; meaning is carried by fill colour and motion.

## 15. The small truths a builder gets wrong
- Fills follow REAL borders and coastlines; a blob is only used for a made-up region.
- Anything that points at something is attached to the target in map coordinates, so camera moves do not make it miss.
- The same map continues through the whole Short; do not rebuild it per scene, move the camera over it. The camera never leaves the map except for the one final reveal insert.
- A route line is drawn from where the last one ended; never restart it. The dot sits on its end.
- Captions come from the voice's word timing; the key word is yellow and is a noun or number, not "the".
- Place name labels are small and plain; the country name on a fill is huge and bold.
- Text sits on plain ocean or fill, never over the caption band, never over a face.
- Icons are flat, one hue plus white, never 3D, never with soft shadows beyond one small drop.
- The final place or answer is shown in the last sentence, not earlier; the hook never names it.
- Persons: a hexagon badge only for a real person who agreed, and figures are pictograms.
- Anything counting (people, ships, cars) is shown as 3-6 small copies, not 50.
- Sound hits land on the graphic's arrival frame, not on the camera start.

## 17. Whole channel: script formula (top 20 % = 161 Shorts, median 163 K views; the rest 648, median 14 K)
- **Length**: winners median 75 s, the rest 69 s. Views by length bucket (median): 45-60 s 11 K, 60-75 s 18 K (461 Shorts, the bulk), 75-90 s 29 K, 90-105 s 25 K, 105+ s 37 K (few). **Longer wins: aim 70-85 s**, never under 55 s.
- **Words**: winners median 224 words, 17 sentences, 2.99 words/s (rest 198 words, 14 sentences, 2.89). Over the 120 measured: 2.46-3.67 words/s. So **about 215-235 words for 75 s**, 16-18 sentences of 10-16 words.
- **First sentence**: winners median 16 words (rest 18), 4-8 s spoken. Opening patterns among the winners (n=161): "Did you know ..." 42 %, "What would happen ..." 20 %, "What if the/all/every ..." about 7 %, "Most people don't / have no idea ..." about 5 %, "Why the hell (does/is) ..." / "Why no one ..." about 4 %, "You ever wonder ..." / "Do you know ..." 3 %. Among the rest, list/ranking openers ("Here are the ...", "Countries with the most ...", "This is the ...") are 3x more common: **list openers lose; one odd fact or one big what-if wins**.
- Only 29 % of winning hooks contain a digit (rest 33 %): the hook is a claim or a scenario, the number comes in sentence 2-4.
- **Hook formula (pick one)**: (a) `Did you know [place] [absurd single fact]?` (b) `What would happen if [huge physical event] [at a place or for a time]?` (c) `What if [one country/continent/resource] [vanished/united/changed]?` (d) `Most people have no idea that there was once a real plan to ...` (e) `Why the hell is [familiar thing] [odd]?` (f) person: `This man spent [N years] [extreme act] with one rule`. The hook never names the answer.
- **Themes of the top 20**: world-scale disasters and what-ifs (a dam collapses, all water gone, countries vanish), lost/phantom/forbidden places (a country only on maps, a no-fly zone, an island where you cannot die), mega-projects that never happened, one canal/strait choke point, "why is [country thing] different", election what-ifs. Dry state-by-state lists with no story (salary after tax, "how far can we dig") sit in the bottom.
- **Ending (last sentence)**, three kinds about equal: (1) an open question ("would you dare make that walk?", "would you want to live in that world?"), (2) a flat final consequence ("humanity wouldn't stand a chance", "nobody can predict when"), (3) a named answer or place. One short sentence, 6-14 words, after a 0.3-0.5 s pause. No "subscribe", no "thanks for watching".
- **Structure for 75 s**: hook 0-6 s; context 6-18 s (who/where/when, one number); the chain 18-62 s (5-8 steps, each one place or one cause-effect with a number, joined by "and then", "but", "so"); the turn 62-70 s ("but here's the catch", "and nobody knows why"); last line 70-75 s.
- **Titles**: winners' titles are questions 47 % (rest 27 %), median 11 words; `#shorts` in the title only 9 % of winners (rest 42 %). Formulas: `Why [odd thing about a country]?`, `What would happen if [event]?`, `[Odd place]: [its name]`, `Did you know ...` statements. No emoji, no ALL CAPS, hashtags go in the description. Title = the hook sentence, shortened.
- Why: the viewer decides in 2 s; an absurd single fact plus an open question gives a reason to stay 70 s; length wins because each extra 10 s of chained places is another map fact the viewer is paid.

## 18. Whole channel: the camera (120 Shorts, 2,516 measured moves; 601 labelled frames)
- **Zoom levels, share of frames**: region 37.4 %, continent 28.1 %, country 12.3 %, world 9.8 %, street 5.7 %, city 4.2 %, none (full-screen graphic) 2.5 %. **Two thirds of the Short is region or continent scale**; world, street, city together 20 %. A Short uses 4-5 levels and never stays at one for more than about 12 s.
- **Camera behaviour, share of frames**: slow push-in 30.6 %, hold 23.3 %, zoom-in 15.6 %, zoom-out 15.0 %, pan 7.2 %, 3D tilt 5.2 %, fly between two places 2.3 %, rotate 0.8 %. The camera moves slowly in more than half of all frames.
- **Moves**: median 18 per Short, 1 hard cut median. Duration median 0.53 s, p90 1.3 s. Peak speed median 3.45 % of frame width per frame, reached at 50 % of the move (symmetric ease in-out). Whole-Short speed percentiles: p50 0.02, p90 1.8, p99 7.6 % of width per frame. Jitter median 0.41 % of width, rotation p95 0.35 deg per frame.
- **Slow holds**: push-in 0.15-0.25 % scale per frame (about 6 % per second), pull-out the same; drift 0.0-0.2 % of width per frame.
- **How it travels between places**: (1) the standard **fly** = fast zoom OUT to a level holding both places (0.4-0.6 s), pan (0.3-0.5 s), zoom IN (0.4-0.7 s), overlapped, 0.7-1.3 s total (about 80 %); (2) a direct pan when both are in one region-level frame (0.3-0.5 s, about 10 %); (3) along a route, the camera follows the line's tip; (4) a dissolve for a scale jump (1.7 per Short, 0.13-0.8 s): the same place at two zooms.
- **3D tilt (5 %)**: the map card tips back so the ground recedes toward the top (8-15 degrees), used in the hook, for a person's pin and for "world as a card on a blueprint grid"; 1-3 s, then flattens.
- **Anchor position**: x 0.50, y 0.38-0.48; caption band is the lower third (captions' centre y median 0.677, range 0.63-0.77).
- **Moves per sentence by role**: hook 1.7, explanation 1.15, punchline 0.86, shock 0.8, tension 0.45; motion level (0-255 frame difference) hook 5.8, tension 4.9, explanation 4.7, shock and punchline 3.8.
- Never a dead frame: near-still share median 23 % (range 7-77 %). Our target under 20 %, and never over 1 s with nothing moving.
- Why: zoom in = "right here", zoom out = "how big, compared to the world", slow push = attention held while the fact is told, fly = "and then".

## 19. Whole channel: map modes and highlights
- **Map modes** (601 frames): satellite 83.4 %, flat colour map 7.2 %, dark vector map 4.3 %, non-map graphic 3.3 %, globe 1.8 %.
  - **Satellite** (default): true colour, deep teal ocean, tan/green land, white ice. Everything physical: places, routes, disasters, what-ifs, borders.
  - **Flat colour map**: political map, countries in flat colours with thin 1-2 px white borders, no terrain; when the sentence is about MANY countries or states at once. 3-5 colours from the palette.
  - **Dark vector map**: near-black ground (#0A1A12 to #0B1520), thin light lines for borders and coasts, one neon accent (cyan #2EC7C0, green #7CFF3A or yellow #FFE600): networks, flights, cables, secrets, night/lights what-ifs.
  - **Globe**: a rotating sphere, 1-2 s, for a world-scale hook or event; never the main stage.
  - **Non-map**: a full-frame graphic (chart, timeline, clipping, an object on a blueprint-blue grid) for under 3 s.
- **How a region is highlighted** (share of 601 frames; styles combine): solid fill 74 %, glow edge 35 %, pattern 18 %, dim-the-rest 17 %, outline only 11 %, 3D extrusion 0.3 % (never use it).
  - **Solid fill**: flat colour, 80-92 % opaque, hard edge on the real border, terrain faintly visible. Most used: white #FFFFFF 27 % of highlights (neutral or "ghost" place, often with a dark edge), reds #B22234 / #C8102E / #D62A2E / #8B1018 about 17 % (country, obstacle, danger), oranges #E8641A / #E8650F / #E8922A / #F5B21E about 8 % (subject), yellows #FFE600 / #FFEE00 / #F5E610 about 5 % (key area, route), greens #3FD83F / #2E9E4F / #7CFF3A about 3 % (allowed, safe), cyan-teal #2EC7C0 about 1 % (water).
  - **Glow edge**: thin 3-6 px bright edge (cyan #5FE8DC for water bodies, white for land) with 6-12 px soft outer glow, fill 40-55 % opaque. Used on seas, lakes, straits. On land use plain fill, not glow.
  - **Pattern**: diagonal hatch or dots, 25-40 % opacity, for disputed, restricted, drained or lost areas.
  - **Dim the rest**: map outside the subject to brightness about 0.35 and saturation 0.3, subject untouched; "only this one".
  - **Outline**: 3-4 px white or yellow on the real border, no fill, when the terrain beneath must stay readable (a river, a coast).
- Max 3 fill colours per frame; the subject is one warm colour; for two competing subjects one is red and the other white or blue.
- A highlight arrives by growing from its point (or capital) outward in 0.4-0.7 s, or wiping along the border; a lost/flooded area hatches or darkens in 0.6 s.

## 20. Whole channel: routes, pins, photos, labels, badges, stickers, clippings, charts, character, transitions
- **Route line (17 % of frames)**: yellow #FFE600 (also #FFE800 / #FFEE00 / #FFEB00), 5-8 px at 1080 wide, round caps. Others: white 4 %, blue #2D9CF0, neon green #39FF14, teal #5FE8DC, red #FF5A4F for danger or an enemy route. Glow is rare: a 2-3 px halo of the same colour, never a bloom. **Dashed** for a plan, a flight or "might happen": 14 px dash, 10 px gap. Draws on from the previous end at the sentence's speed (a short hop 0.5 s; a world crossing 1.5-2.5 s) and the camera follows the tip. Wide band arrows (flat two-stop gradient yellow to green, 120-180 px wide, arrow head) show flows (water, migration) in 7 % of frames: flat band, not a 3D arrow.
- **Moving dot (6 %)**: 14-18 px yellow dot, white 3 px ring, on the line's end; a ring pulse (14 -> 60 px, fading, 0.6 s) on arrival at a place. For transport topics a small flat plane/ship/car icon (8-10 % of width) is the dot.
- **Pin (14 %)**: flat teardrop, 6-8 % of width, red #D93A32 or yellow, white 3 px ring, one small drop shadow; drops in with a 0.2 s bounce (10 % overshoot). **Photo pin (4 %)**: circle or hexagon 14-17 % of width, white 4 px border, tail pointing at the spot; slides up 0.25 s, sits 2-5 s.
- **Place labels (21 %)**: white bold rounded-geometric sans, 3.5-4.5 % of width tall, dark 2 px outline plus soft shadow, an **underline leader** (3 px white line from the text to the target, ending in a dot) is the commonest style; or a thin vertical stem. **Region labels (33 %)**: country/sea names 8-14 % of width, heavy white on the fill with a dark soft shadow, at the centroid, rotated to follow a narrow shape (river, strait) up to 30 deg, sometimes with a smaller second line.
- **Flags (26 % of frames)**: flat 3:2 rectangle 4-7 % of width, 2 px white border, next to a country name or on its fill, pops in with the label in 0.15 s. Needs an ISO-country flag set.
- **Title text (25.5 %)**: at the top (y 0.12-0.30): white heavy sans on a black box; yellow heavy with black outline and a coloured bullet (red/blue) on list beats; yellow caps on a red plate; red tilted badge with white edge. Typed word by word with the voice, replaced not stacked. Cyan rounded text with soft glow only on dark-vector frames.
- **Number badges (12.5 %)**: number 10-16 % of width tall, white heavy or yellow with black outline, on a red or orange pill, tilted 3-8 deg, count-up 0.6-1.0 s for money, people, years, percent; beside the thing it measures, never on the caption band, unit smaller below; a clock-icon pill for time.
- **Icon stickers (44 % of frames, the most common overlay)**: flat vector, one or two hues, thick white 4-6 px outline (sticker look), 8-16 % of width, 1-3 per frame. Kinds: light bulb, camera, plane, ship, container ship, car, dam, wave, fire, skull, money bag and notes, coin, people figures, lock, key, gavel, document, radiation sign, house, tree, thermometer, clock, rocket, oil barrel, crown, and **a flat peach hand holding the object**, entering from the frame edge at 20-40 % of width. They sit ON the place the noun names, or in free ocean, or come in from the edge. Pop in at the word (scale 0 -> 1.12 -> 1.0 in 0.2 s) with one sound hit, bob 2 px, leave in 0.15 s when the next noun arrives. Max 3 at once, never over the highlight's centre.
- **Callout bubbles (4.8 %)**: white rounded box, black text, small tail to the target, 18-30 % of width; a quote or aside.
- **News clippings 1.3 %, charts 1 %, timelines 0.7 %**: clipping = torn-paper card, serif headline, one yellow-highlighted line, in 0.3 s, 2-4 s; chart = flat bar/line on a dark card, 1-2 colours, bars grow in 0.6 s; timeline = thin horizontal line with 3-5 dated dots in the upper third, the one case of a still camera (3-6 s).
- **Arrows (7 %)**: thin white arrow from a label to a target, or a thick flat arrow for a direction; always attached to its target.
- **Grid (5 %)**: the world as a flat card, tilted 8-15 degrees, over a blueprint-blue grid (about #2D6CC0, lighter 1 px lines, 40 px squares, slow drift): the hook and the "zoom out to the whole world" beat.
- **Characters (13 % of frames, heads only in person stories)**: hexagon-photo pin for a person; flat white/peach figures (3-6); a flat person cropped at the bottom for a question; emoji faces. No animation beyond pop-in and bob.
- **Transitions**: fly (about 80 %), direct pan (10 %), dissolve for scale jumps (1.7 per Short), rare whip (0.3 s directional blur). Hard cuts median 1 per Short, used when the map layer changes mode (satellite -> dark vector). Never a fade to black.

## 21. Whole channel: sound, pacing, density and the WHY
- **Voice** median 159 Hz (88-213), about -14 dB RMS; music bed about 13 dB under the voice (6-27); narration from 0.0 s; almost no gaps. Captions on screen 99 % (median), centre y 0.677, glyph band about 24 px at 1080, 6.8 changes per second, change = word start, yellow key word.
- **Sound hits**: median 1.06 per second over the 120 (the first six had 1.3-1.9). Build to **1.2 per second**, inside words, on the graphic's arrival frame; booms and clicks mostly.
- **Density**: 3-5 assets per shot (map + highlight + label + 1-2 stickers), 1 anchor, overlays per frame median 2, max 4.
- **Look**: saturation median 0.59 (0.15-0.88), value 0.43, vignette edge ratio 0.76, grain about 7 of 255, centre sharpness (about 1,800) far above the edges (about 450): the edges soften and darken.
- **Sentence roles and treatment**: explanation (43 %) = fill + label; hook (7 %) = tilt or tight close-up pulling back + first noun graphic at 0.3 s; shock/consequence (16 %) = number badge or skull/fire sticker + zoom-in; tension (16 %) = slow push-in with almost nothing new; punchline (8 %) = pull out to the world and one last graphic; comedy beat (4 %) = emoji or hand sticker.
- **Colour means something**: red = danger/obstacle/the other side, orange/yellow = subject and route, green = allowed/safe, white = neutral or "lost" place, cyan = water, blue grid = "the whole world as an object". Never decorative.
- **Every graphic is tied to a word**: it appears on the word's frame, leaves when the next idea comes (life 1.5-3 s), and a camera move is tied to a change of place in the sentence.

## 22. Better than the reference (what the channel does weakly, and how we beat it)
1. **Low-resolution satellite zoomed in too far** (edge sharpness near zero in many Shorts; street and city frames smeared). Ours: base map in 4K tiles per region, never zoom past 1 base texel per screen pixel; street scale only with a real street tile, otherwise stop at region/country and use a crisp vector overlay.
2. **Clutter**: frames with 5-7 overlays, labels over labels, stickers on the fill's centre, captions over icons. Ours: max 4 overlays, one anchor, a label solver with keep-out zones for the caption band (y 0.60-0.80) and headline bar (y 0.10-0.30).
3. **Repeated assets** (same bulb, skull, money, hand over and over). Ours: 120+ icons in one outline style, never the same icon twice in a Short, chosen by the noun.
4. **Dead holds** (near-still up to 77 %, long timeline holds). Ours: every hold has a push-in of 0.15-0.25 %/frame plus a moving element (dot, ring pulse, count-up); near-still under 20 %.
5. **Unreadable text**: labels under 3 % of width, white on pale sand or ice, captions over busy terrain. Ours: labels at least 4 % of width with 2 px outline and soft shadow, dark text automatically on white fills, caption outline 3 px plus 8 px soft shadow, a 20 % brightness drop in the map behind the caption band.
6. **Fills off the real border, glow on land**. Ours: real polygons (Natural Earth), anti-aliased edges, glow only on water.
7. **Inconsistent grade** (saturation 0.15-0.88). Ours: one grade for all Shorts: sat 0.6, value 0.43, vignette 0.8, grain 7.
8. **Weak openers and endings** (list openers lose). Ours: a hook of type (a)-(f), ending a question or flat consequence, a question title.
9. **Sparse sound**. Ours: 1.2 hits per second on arrivals plus a quiet whoosh (-24 dB) under every fly, so the camera is heard without a hit on the cut.
10. **Camera that stops dead**. Ours: ease-in-out with peak at 50 % and a 2-frame settle, never linear.
Stays in the style: satellite stage, fill on a muted map, yellow route and dot, captions with a yellow key word, tilted red headline plate, flat sticker icons, colour = meaning.

## 23. Map engine needs (build list with numbers)
**A. Base map**
1. Satellite tiles (true colour), world coverage in 8-16K equirectangular or web-mercator tiles; camera = (lon, lat, zoom, tilt 0-15 deg); zoom from world (360 deg across) to country (about 8 deg) to city (about 0.2 deg), never below base texel density.
2. Grade: saturation 0.6, value 0.43, vignette edge 0.8, grain 7/255, soft edges (0.6 px blur at the border of the frame).
3. Modes: `satellite`, `flat`, `dark`, `globe`, `card` (world as a flat tilted card on a blue grid), `none`. White polar ice and the blue grid tile at the map's edge are allowed.
**B. Camera director**
4. Ease-in-out keyframes; `fly(a,b)` = zoom out 0.4-0.6 s, pan 0.3-0.5 s, zoom in 0.4-0.7 s, overlapped, 0.7-1.3 s; `pan` 0.3-0.5 s; `push(dur, 0.2 %/frame)`; `pull`; `tilt(deg, dur)`; `follow(route)`; `dissolve(0.13-0.8 s)`.
5. Rules: about one move per 4 s (18 per 75 s), push-in on every hold, 4-5 zoom levels, region+continent 65 % of the time, no level over 12 s.
**C. Highlights**
6. `fill(polygon, colour, 0.85, grow 0.4-0.7 s)`, `outline(polygon, colour, 3-4 px)`, `glow(polygon, #5FE8DC, 4 px + 10 px halo, water only)`, `hatch(polygon, 45 deg, 25-40 %)`, `dim(rest, 0.35)`; max 3 fills per frame; polygons from Natural Earth admin-0/1, lakes, seas.
**D. Lines and markers**
7. `route(points, #FFE600, 6 px, round, draw-on speed, solid | dashed 14/10)`, `dot(end, 16 px, 3 px white ring, arrival pulse)`, `arrow(label, target, 3 px white)`, `band(path, 150 px, 2-colour, head)`, `vehicle(icon, route)`.
8. `pin(lon, lat, colour, 7 % width, bounce 10 %)`, `photo_pin(image, circle|hexagon, 16 % width, white 4 px)`.
**E. Text**
9. Captions from word timings (2-6 words, y 0.677, yellow key word, 3 px outline + soft shadow, 6-7 changes per s).
10. `headline(text, black-bar | red-plate-tilted | yellow-outline, typed per word, y 0.12-0.30)`.
11. `place_label` (4 % width, underline leader with dot), `region_label` (8-14 % width on the fill, rotate to shape up to 30 deg), `flag(iso, 5 % width)`, `number_badge(value, unit, count-up 0.6-1.0 s, tilt 3-8 deg, red/orange pill)`, `callout(text, tail)`.
12. Label placement solver: keep-out for caption band and headline bar, never over the anchor centre, auto dark text on white.
**F. Stickers and inserts**
13. Sticker library (flat vector, 1-2 hues, 4-6 px white outline, 120+ icons), `sticker(noun, pos, 8-16 %, pop 0 -> 1.12 -> 1 in 0.2 s, bob 2 px, life 1.5-3 s, max 3)`, `hand_holding(icon)` from the frame edge at 20-40 % width.
14. `clipping(headline, highlighted line)`, `chart(bar|line, 1-2 colours, grow 0.6 s)`, `timeline(3-5 dates, still camera 3-6 s)`, `emoji(face)`, `figures(3-6)`.
**G. Sound**
15. Voice + music bed 13 dB under it + hits at 1.2 per second on each graphic's arrival frame (boom/click/thump), ducking in pauses, a quiet whoosh (-24 dB) under every fly. Never a hit on the cut.
**H. Script**
16. Planner writes 215-235 words in 16-18 sentences, a hook from section 17, one place or number per sentence, an ending question or consequence, 70-85 s; and a per-sentence shot plan `{words, place, camera_move, highlight, overlay[], sound}`.

## 24. The creator's rules after the first real Short (2026-10-27; wins over EVERYTHING above)
The Suez Short was "too chaotic", "not clean", and had "2 subtitles, which makes no sense". So:
- **One line of words on screen, ever.** Legion burns captions of every spoken word at the bottom. The typed headline bar NEVER
  repeats what the voice is saying. It is used at most twice per Short: the title in the hook (the question, 3-7 words, typed in
  the first 2 s, gone by 4 s) and, if needed, one chapter word at the turn ("BUT", "DAY 30"). Number plates carry a number or
  a unit only ("12%", "+10 days"), never a sentence. No other text over the map except place names.
- **Calm.** At most 2 overlays at once on top of the map and its one highlight (a plate + a sticker, or a route + a label).
  Each overlay lives at least 2 s and leaves (eased out) before the next one comes; never a pile. About one new element per
  sentence, not per word. 6-8 number plates in 60 s at most, 4-6 stickers, 1 insert. Pulses only on arrivals.
- **Clean motion.** Every overlay enters with `MAP.life` (0.35 s glide in, small scale from 0.85, no bounce) and leaves with it
  (0.25 s fade out); routes draw with an ease-in-out over at least 0.8 s; the camera makes one move per place change (fly 0.9-1.3
  s), and between moves it only pushes slowly. No shake, no flicker, no element snapping in on a hard frame.
- **Bigger and fewer.** Labels at least 4.5 % of the width, stickers at least 9 %, plates at least 6 % tall; nothing tiny.
- Counts never show a zero frame: a counting plate starts at the first non-zero value it can show (`count: [from, to, p]` with
  from > 0) or appears already counting.
